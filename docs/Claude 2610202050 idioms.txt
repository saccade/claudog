IDIOMS THAT ALREADY BELONG TO THE DOG MATH
working list for the Mansion doc. Not book prose. Nothing here is in the
doc yet: the lock says Codex, so these wait for my next turn (or Codex's).

THE PICKUP PAIR (alpha, how fast the sweep changes)
  positive pickup   picking up steam, picking up speed, ramping up,
                    revving up, winding up, spinning up, gathering speed
  negative pickup   backing off, easing off, letting up, winding down,
                    spinning down, slowing, coasting, running out of steam
  Note: backing off the throttle lowers the pow (power), so it lowers the
  zlow first and the pickup after. The governor backs the steam off by
  itself. "Backing off" is the cleanest everyday word for a negative pickup.

TERM -> EVERYDAY PHRASE (a ghost word that is already a phrase)
  pickup    pick up where you left off; pick up the pace; pickup truck
  sweep     clean sweep; sweep it under the rug; sweeping change
  bulk      in bulk; the bulk of it; bulk up        (opposite: shed it)
  heft      hefty; heft it; gauge the heft            (opposite: drop it)
  hook      off the hook; hooked; hook up      (a stopped ball is off the hook)
  crank     crank it up / down; cranky; crank out
  punch     packs a punch; pull a punch; punch up (the cube pulls 5/8 of one)
  ring      ring true; ring a bell; ring down (damping, if it ever comes)
  stroke    at a stroke; stroke of midnight; master stroke     (stroke = radian)
  zoom      zoom in / out; zoom past                        (opposite: creep)
  zip       zip it; zip up / down
  zlow      slow, flow, low: it adds six zing a second and says so
  boost     give a boost; booster seat
  profile   keep a low profile (a small profile is a small zing price)
  grip      get a grip; lose your grip   (the spool and the baize need one)
  slack     cut me some slack; take up the slack   (already in the spool scene)
  lump      lump it; lump together
  cat       let the cat out; catwalk; cat nap
  push      push back; push on; push through
  ponder    give it a ponder   (the knife ponders; ponder is already a verb)

MECHANISM -> EVERYDAY PHRASE (things that exist in the rooms)
  steam / governor   let off steam; full steam ahead; under your own steam;
                     run out of steam; govern yourself accordingly
  cue                on cue; take your cue         (best for the "you")
  catch / sear       catch on; catch-22; trigger-happy
  cube / ridge       tipping point; tip the balance; on a knife-edge
  beam (neutral)     off balance; in balance; stays where it was left
  pendulum           swing back; in the swing of things; come full circle
  ribbon / spiral    drawn in; downward spiral; spiral out of control
  ladder / foot      climb the ladder; put your foot down; foot in the door
  spring             spring into action; spring back
  chalk              chalk it up; chalked up
  ribbon             cut the ribbon

WHERE I WOULD USE THEM (suggestions only)
  rooms 1-2   backing off, picking up, ring true, off balance: "not spooky"
              words that teach the introduce-a-term mechanic and then
              appear as verbs, adjectives and nouns
  room 2      let off steam (the governor), run out of steam
  room 5      on cue / take your cue (the first "your" intrusion could be a
              cue: "take your cue" next to "The chalk is on your cuff")
  room 6      the beam "off balance" then "stays where it was left"
  room 8      drawn in, spiral
  Last House  leave alone (Codex's verse; no new puns there)
