DOG MATH
========

Written by Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-19 16:12. The dog math rules
and units as they stand today, gathered from the notes in the
Straight-> Mechanics draft and dr. kraemer's rulings in chat. Not
doc's text; the rules are his, these words are the model's.

1. What dog math is
-------------------

Dog math (dog physics is fine too) is playing with ideas on
purpose. When hands, cats, dogs, lumps, thumps, pow and zoom are
on the board, the work is real science or real engineering, but
it says out loud that it isn't the final job and won't get lost
in the weeds.

It is the default for all the work unless doc says something is
for outside eyes.

2. The rules
------------

1.  Round hard. Gravity is 10 zoom (dog gravity). Anything close
    to a round number is that round number. A mole is 3/5 of a
    trillion trillion.

2.  A result should survive rounding, dropping or scrambling
    every digit past about the third. If 96 against 100 matters,
    you're measuring the wrong thing: pick a simpler example or
    find the real quantity.

3.  The principal job is naming quantities that mean things,
    saying how to measure or construct them, and relating them in
    sentences that don't turn around on themselves.

4.  A name has to hold up against its dictionary meaning
    everywhere you can see it mapped. No calling a force a carrot
    just to say you measured something.

5.  Push the language to the minimum while playing. Drop dead
    words, take shortenings, have fun with units, and don't police
    anyone's coinages. Formalize later, and only where it would
    step on toes.

6.  Choose the sizes so the units eat the algebra. Radii in whole
    hands make every profile a whole number of cards; wherever
    there seems to be algebra, a unit on the board will usually
    eat most of it.

7.  Local units are fine. A problem may define its own unit at the
    top when it makes the numbers whole: 1 mw is a milliwheel, so
    a lump at one card is 10 mw of inertia.

8.  Every quantity gets a one-word unit: its own name unless a
    named unit already exists. You don't say "inertia" after a
    number; you say "wheels".

9.  For outside eyes ("for college", "for high school") the dog
    units stay in the dog park. The other rules still hold: no
    third decimal, and name things.

3. The dog units
----------------

+-------------+------------------------------+-----------------+
| Unit        | What it is                   | Standard        |
+-------------+------------------------------+-----------------+
| hand        | about a hand's width         | 10 cm (~4 in)   |
| card        | a square hand; about a 3x5   | 0.01 m^2        |
|             | index card                   |                 |
| lump        | the unit of mass             | 1 kg            |
| cat         | the unit of speed            | 1 m/s           |
| dog         | ten cats                     | 10 m/s          |
| zoom        | a cat per second             | 1 m/s^2         |
| dog gravity | a dog per second, down       | 10 zoom         |
| push        | force: an oompf per second   | 1 N             |
| press       | pressure: a push per square  | 1 Pa            |
|             | metre (Pa for company)       |                 |
| thump       | a lump lifted a hand in dog  | 1 J             |
|             | gravity                      |                 |
| pow         | a thump per second           | 1 W             |
| crank       | torque: a push at a metre,   | 1 N m           |
|             | an arrow along the axis      |                 |
| grunt       | trundle: a thump per stroke, | 1 J per stroke  |
|             | a plain amount               |                 |
+-------------+------------------------------+-----------------+

4. The book's own units
-----------------------

These aren't play units; they are the one-word units of quantities
the book assigns, and they stay in any work:

+-------------+------------------------------+-----------------+
| Unit        | Quantity it measures         | Standard        |
+-------------+------------------------------+-----------------+
| oompf       | motion                       | 1 kg m/s        |
| stroke      | swing (one radian's worth)   | 1 rad           |
| sweep       | sweep: a stroke per second   | 1 rad/s         |
| whirl       | pickup: a sweep per second   | 1 rad/s^2       |
| ponder      | bulk ("a ponderous bulk")    | 1 kg m          |
| wheel       | inertia                      | 1 kg m^2        |
| roll        | spin                         | 1 kg m^2/s      |
| zip         | zip: punch per mass          | 1 J/kg          |
| zing        | zing: hook per inertia       | 1 J/(kg m^2)    |
| zug, zow,   | work, power, action: per mass| per mass or     |
| zam         | straight, per inertia turning| per inertia     |
| shift,      | their own quantities         | 1 kg m          |
| transit     |                              |                 |
+-------------+------------------------------+-----------------+

5. Handy facts
--------------

        1 metre        = 10 hands
        1 wheel        = a lump at 100 cards
        a lump at a card = 10 mw (local unit)
        1 thump        = a lump lifted a hand in dog gravity
        1 crank through 1 stroke = 1 grunt
        dog gravity    = 10 zoom
        a turn         = about 6.3 strokes
        a 3x5 card     = within a few percent of a card
