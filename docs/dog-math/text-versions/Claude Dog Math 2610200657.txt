DOG MATH
========

Written by Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-20 06:57. The dog math
rules and units as they stand today, gathered from the notes in
the straight-> mechanics draft and dr. kraemer's rulings in chat,
with how to verb the ghosts, ghost manners and stealth mode. Not
doc's text; the rules are his, these words are the model's.

1. What dog math is
-------------------

Dog math (dog physics is fine too) is playing with ideas on
purpose. When hands, cats, dogs, lumps, thumps, pow and zoom are
on the board, the work is real science or real engineering, but
it says out loud that it isn't the final job and won't get lost
in the weeds.

It is the default for all the work unless doc says something is
for outside eyes.

2. The rules
------------

1.  Round hard. Gravity is 10 zoom (dog gravity). Anything close
    to a round number is that round number. A mole is 3/5 of a
    trillion trillion.

2.  A result should survive rounding, dropping or scrambling
    every digit past about the third. If 96 against 100 matters,
    you're measuring the wrong thing: pick a simpler example or
    find the real quantity.

3.  The principal job is naming quantities that mean things,
    saying how to measure or construct them, and relating them in
    sentences that don't turn around on themselves.

4.  A lump of algebra, or words that tangle or run long, usually
    means a quantity hasn't been named yet. Find the body or
    setup property it describes, say how to measure or construct
    it, and let that quantity collapse the algebra. The quantity
    does the structural work; friendly units make its numbers
    easy.

5.  A name has to hold up against its dictionary meaning
    everywhere you can see it mapped. No calling a force a carrot
    just to say you measured something.

6.  One name per thing, one thing per name. That includes the
    word's ordinary uses: in plain English "spinning at 6" names
    the sweep, so spin has to be kept clear of it. Assigning a
    word never bans its everyday sense; no breaking the world.

7.  Make the words work hard while playing. A quantity can go
    adjectival (a ponderous bulk), adverbial, or get verbed (the
    drum picks up; a lever sweeps force). Drop dead words, skip
    words outright, take shortenings, have fun with units, and
    don't police anyone's coinages. It's okay as long as everyone
    is having fun. Formalize later, and only where it would step
    on toes.

8.  Choose the sizes so the units eat the algebra. Radii in whole
    hands make the radius's reflex a whole number of cards;
    wherever there seems to be algebra, a unit on the board will
    usually eat most of it.

9.  Local units are fine. A problem may define its own unit at the
    top when it makes the numbers whole: 1 mw is a milliwheel, so
    a lump at a hand, at a hand, is 10 mw of inertia.

10. Every quantity gets a one-word unit: its own name unless a
    named unit already exists. You don't say "inertia" after a
    number; you say "wheel".

11. The dog units take no plural s: 5.3 hand, 7 lump, 14 push.
    5.3 hands is a trip to the hospital.

12. For outside eyes ("for college", "for high school") the dog
    units stay in the dog park. The other rules still hold: no
    third decimal, and name things.

5. Handy facts
--------------

    1 metre                     = 10 hand
    1 wheel                     = a ponder at a metre
    a lump at a hand, at a hand = 10 mw (local unit)
    1 thump                     = a lump lifted a hand, dog gravity
    1 crank through 1 stroke    = 1 grunt
    dog gravity                 = 10 zoom
    a turn                      = about 6.3 stroke
    a 3x5 card                  = within a few percent of a card

6. Ghosts
---------

A ghost is a quantity with the body taken out. Straight ghosts
come per lump; turning ghosts come per wheel. Some turning
quantities also have a per-lump ghost.

+--------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+
|              | per lump (mass out)   | per wheel (inertia    |
|              |                       | out)                  |
+--------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+
| oompf        | velocity              |                       |
| push         | acceleration          |                       |
| thump, punch | zip                   |                       |
| wheel        | profile               |                       |
| roll         | swirl                 | sweep                 |
| crank        | twirl                 | pickup                |
| thump, hook  | zump                  | zing                  |
| thump, work  | zug                   | zlug                  |
| pow          | zow                   | zlow                  |
+--------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+

Take the wheel out, with half a wheel:

    3 roll on half a wheel sweeps at 6.
    2 crank on half a wheel picks up at 4.
    9 thump of hook on half a wheel is 18 zing.
    4 pickup over 4.5 stroke is 18 zlug.
    4 pickup at 6 sweep is 24 zlow.

Take the lump out instead, with the 2-lump ball at 5 hand:

    half a wheel on 2 lump is 25 card of profile.
    9 thump of hook on 2 lump is 4.5 zump.
    25 card of profile across 18 zing is 4.5 zump.

Spin and torque per wheel leave the motion itself, sweep and
pickup, just as movement and push per lump leave the velocity and
the acceleration. Spin on the wheel gives the sweep wherever the
setup fixes the axis. A crank on the wheel gives the pickup only
while the inertia stays put: a skater tucking on a fixed axis
picks up with no crank at all.

Ghosts take the passive voice. The point is to pass them around
without anything in particular having them: 18 zing is added,
not "it gains 18 zing". Or let the quantity act: 4 pickup over
4.5 stroke adds 18 zing. There's still a ghost body back there,
but it's rude to point at it. Put a body back across a ghost and
the active voice comes back with it: half a wheel with 18 zing
hooks 9 thump. The passive is a way of writing, not a claim about
the physics.

The same goes for the units. Whirl is the pickup of a body turning
on itself, so it belongs where the body is in the sentence: 0.8
crank on 40 mw picks the puck up at 20 whirl. In a ghost sentence
there's no body to whirl, so it's pickup, even for a puck: 20
pickup over 10 stroke adds 200 zing.

Ghosts carry no mass, so they walk through walls, and sometimes
they let you follow them if you dare. People get lost in those
walls. They say if you could see into the dark in there, it would
be nothing but algebra, everywhere; be grateful the lights are
off. The way out is always the same: put the body back across.
You go in after 18 zing, and on the far side half a wheel steps
out and hooks 9 thump. The ones who get lost forgot to bring the
mass back.

Some ghosts we're so used to we've stopped seeing them: velocity,
speed, acceleration. One way to sort them: real quantities add,
ghosts average, each by its own weight, the mass for the per-lump
ghosts and the inertia for the per-wheel ones. Glue a 2-lump ball
at 3 cat to a 1-lump ball at 6 cat, both moving the same way:

    movement:  6 oompf and 6 oompf make 12 oompf. It adds.
    velocity:  3 cat and 6 cat make 4 cat, the 12 oompf spread
               across 3 lump. It averages, by the mass.

That's bookkeeping, not a rule about what an interaction changes.
Gluing is a collision. Movement comes through it, so the velocity
comes out as the mass-weighted average. Punch doesn't all come
through:

    before:  27 thump of punch, 9 zip
    after:   24 thump at 4 cat, 8 zip; 3 thump burned

The zip isn't the old average, because some of the punch is gone.

Movement, spin, punch and hook are carried by bodies, and can be
split up and swapped around between them. Velocity, speed, zip and
acceleration belong to no body in particular, and split only by
the mass. Ghosts aren't weak for that. A free body keeps its
velocity, and velocities say where bodies will meet. What they
can't settle alone is how much motion gets traded when bodies
interact: that takes the masses and the rules of the interaction.

Dog gravity is a ghost: 10 pull a lump, 10 zoom. The mass is
already out, so everything falls together. Acceleration is the
ghost of a push and the rate of another ghost, the velocity, which
may be why people find it hard to hold: F = m a hands over a ghost
when there's a push on a real body right in front of them.

The zing row has no short textbook version at all: the textbook
has no name for half the square of the angular velocity. The row
after it is what zing is for: the wheel goes across the zing the
way the mass goes across the profile, and out comes hook.

Zing is a ghost, so no body has it: the pickup adds it, not "it".
Put the wheel across it and there's a body again: half a wheel
with 18 zing hooks 9 thump.

The second row says "half a wheel at 6" and drops "sweeping":
the row above sets up the parallel, so the ear supplies it. That
kind of elision only works where the parallel is that clear. It
can't be done uniformly, and it's impossible with a tongue
covered in algebra.

8. Verbing the ghosts
---------------------

A ghost doesn't have to sit after a number. Let it run. Put a
body, or a slice of one, across a ghost, and it takes the ghost's
verb:

    5 hand sweeping at 6 runs at 3 cat.
    Half a wheel sweeping at 6 hooks 9 thump.
    Half a wheel zinging at 18 hooks 9 thump.
    2 lump zipping at 2 punches 4 thump.
    1 lump dropping a hand hefts 1 thump.

Per lump, the profile takes the same verbs: 25 card sweeping at 6
hooks 4.5 thump a lump.

+-------------+----------------+---------------------------------+
| Ghost       | Its verb       | Said                            |
+-------------+----------------+---------------------------------+
| velocity    | runs at        | the centre runs at 3 cat        |
| acceleration| speeds up at   | the block speeds up at 1.8 zoom |
| sweep       | sweeps at      | 5 hand sweeping at 6            |
| pickup      | picks up at    | the drum picks up at 18         |
| zip         | zips at        | 2 lump zipping at 2             |
| zing        | zings at       | half a wheel zinging at 18      |
| swirl       | swirls         | the ball swirls the same after  |
|             |                | the bounce as before            |
| twirl       | twirls         | a balanced wheel can twirl with |
|             |                | its centre still                |
+-------------+----------------+---------------------------------+

The real quantities have their verbs too: a push pushes, a crank
cranks, a boost boosts, a wheel wheels, a bulk ponders from its
pivot, and a body hooks, punches and hefts. Movement and spin are
carried: the bat carries 2.25 oompf, the rod 1.08 roll.

For outside eyes the verbs still run; only the dog units stay
home. From the ladder that leaves the wall, per kilogram:

+--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| Arithmetic                     | Verbed                         |
+--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| hook about the still point,    | 3.0 m^2 about the still point, |
| (1/2)(3.0)(2.61) = 3.92 J/kg   | sweeping at 1.62, hooks 3.9 J. |
+--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| punch, (1/2)(2.42)^2           | The centre, running at         |
| = 2.94 J/kg                    | 2.42 m/s, punches 2.9 J.       |
+--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
| heft given up,                 | The centre, dropping 0.40 m,   |
| 9.8 (1.2 - 0.8) = 3.92 J/kg    | hefts 3.9 J.                   |
+--------------------------------+--------------------------------+

And from the superball, after its first bounce: 2 R^2 / 5 of
profile about the centre, zinging at 7200 off a sweep of 120,
hooks 1.8 J a kilogram.

Two cautions. Don't force it: where a verb's word hasn't come in
yet, the one that has will carry it, and in the ladder's room,
before zing, "sweeping at" carries the hook. And round as you
verb. A third digit written to the margin goes: 3.92 is 3.9, and
0.533 is about 0.53.

9. Ghost manners
----------------

Mention the ghosts enough that nobody is confused, then leave them
be. Most people are afraid of ghosts, so hold the reader's hand
and make no sudden moves.

  - Don't point. No possessives: "the rod's profile" becomes "the
    profile about the centre". No roll call at the end of each
    problem. No "here's another ghost": name the quantity and let
    it work. "Spin per mass we'll call the swirl."

  - One scary ghost to a problem gets Z. The letter belongs to the
    problem, not the ghost, so Z comes back in every problem as
    something new: "New room, new Z; the last one stayed with the
    rod." The familiar ones, velocity and the sweep, need no
    letter.

  - Say what a ghost is once, plainly, where the reader first
    needs it: a quantity with the body's mass, or its inertia,
    divided out. After that the ghosts can do their work quietly.

10. Stealth mode
----------------

For dogs who need to pass. The dog units stay home (SI, with
oompf, roll, ponder and wheel), and the vocabulary comes in
quietly.

  - Start each problem in the textbook's terms, and end each part
    a little further in. It's "Mary had a little lamb" changing
    key: the same openings, and the endings reveal the new key.

  - Introduce a unit once, in the textbook's terms: "0.072 kg m^2,
    which we'll call 0.072 wheel". Then just use it: the rod
    wheels at 15.

  - Every unit that comes in gets a verb, an adverb or an
    adjective: wheel wheels, crank cranks, hook hooks, a bulk
    ponders from its pivot.

  - A few new names per problem, each in its own sentence, never
    inside the hardest sentence in the room.

  - Read it aloud. If you're chewing through "angular
    acceleration" so often that when another shows up you can
    still taste the last one, it's time to pick up the pace.

  - Introduce the word when the reader is ready to use it. Say the
    textbook's phrase once in full, and let the sentence start
    asking for the name, so "we'll call it the pickup" lands as
    relief. Then it earns its keep at once: the drum picks up,
    picks up faster, stops picking up.

  - Move off sine and cosine and into the arrows by the third
    problem. Name the parts of an arrow once (its upwardness and
    its outwardness, what dividing it by up, and by out, pulls
    from it), and work with those.

  - "Forward around" in full at the first mention in each problem,
    then "around"; "backward around", then "back around".

11. A few problems, said strong
-------------------------------

Three of the working problems, cut down and said the way the
words like to be used. The full workings are in their own files.

The drum picks up

    A wheel turns freely on its axle: a uniform 2-lump disc 2 hand
    in radius, with a uniform 1-lump drum of 1 hand fixed to it on
    the same axle. A rope
    off the drum runs over a smooth post and down to a 1-lump
    block. Let go from rest, how long does the block take to fall
    a metre?

    The profiles go in cards. The disc has half of 4 card, 2 card;
    the drum half of 1 card. The rope makes the block's fall load
    the axle like a lump on the drum's rim, so the block presents
    1 card at the drum. With the mass back across:

        2 lump across 2 card       40 mw
        1 lump across half a card   5 mw
        1 lump across 1 card       10 mw
        the setup                  55 mw

    The block's bulk on the drum is 1 lump at 1 hand, 0.1 ponder.
    Under 10 zoom it cranks at 1. 1 crank on the whole 55 mw picks
    it up at 18, a little over, and 1 hand picking up at 18 speeds
    the block up at 1.8 zoom. A metre from rest takes a second and
    a bit, 1.05; the block lands running at 1.9 cat, the wheel
    sweeping at 19.

    The fall lets go 10 thump of heft, and sweeping at 19 adds 180
    zing. 55 mw with 180 zing hooks 10 thump: the disc and drum's
    45 mw hooks 8.2, the block's 10 mw the other 1.8. The rope
    carries 10 pull less the 1.8 the block keeps, so 8.2 pull
    through the metre puts 8.2 thump into the wheel. Check: 8.2
    pull at 1 hand cranks the wheel's own 45 mw at 0.82, and that
    picks it up at 18 too.

The ball that skids

    A uniform solid bowling ball, 8 lump and 1 hand in radius,
    leaves the hand at 7 cat with no sweep at all. While it skids,
    the lane drags at 16 push. How fast is it going once it rolls?

    Set it up about a line across the lane through the launch
    contact. The drag has no leverage about that line, and the
    weight's and the floor's leverages cancel, so the spin holds.
    8 lump at 1 hand is 0.8 ponder; moving at 7 cat, it has 5.6
    roll, backward around. From here on, back around.

    Moving the line along the lane to the contact once it rolls
    adds no leverage on the movement, so the spin stays 5.6 roll.
    Rolling, the ball turns about its contact: 2/5 card of its own
    profile and 1 card for the centre, 7/5 card. 8 lump across
    7/5 card is 112 mw. 5.6 roll on 112 mw sweeps at 50, and 1 hand
    sweeping at 50 runs at 5 cat. It keeps 5/7 of its pace.

    16 push on 8 lump slows it at 2 zoom, so 7 cat down to 5 takes
    a second; averaging 6, it skids 6 metres. 8 lump moving at
    7 cat punches 196 thump; rolling, 112 mw with 1250 zing hooks
    140. The slip falls steadily from 7 cat to nothing, and 16
    push through the 3.5 metres it rubs burns the other 56. It
    travels 6 metres and rubs 3.5.

The puck on a string

    A uniform 2-lump puck, 2 hand in radius, lies at rest on smooth
    ice with string wound on its rim, leaving on the puck's right.
    A hand steadily pulls at 4, straight ahead along the ice, for
    a second. How far does the hand go, and how much of its work
    ends up as punch?

    Each ledger gets the whole pull. 4 pull on 2 lump speeds the
    centre up at 2 zoom. 4 pull at 2 hand cranks at 0.8, and 0.8
    crank on 40 mw (2 lump across 2 card) picks it up at 20,
    forward around. From here on, around.

    After the second the centre runs at 2 cat, a metre along;
    sweeping at 20, the puck has swung through 10 stroke and paid
    2 metres of string off the rim. The hand went 3.

    As ghosts: 2 zoom over a metre adds 2 zip, and 20 pickup over
    10 stroke adds 200 zing. With the bodies back: 2 lump with
    2 zip punches 4 thump, and 40 mw with 200 zing hooks 8. 4 pull
    through 3 metres adds 12 thump, all of it.

    Punch's share is the profile over the profile and the radius's
    reflex: 2 card over 6 card, a third.
