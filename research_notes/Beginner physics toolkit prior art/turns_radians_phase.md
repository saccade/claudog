# Prior art: angle as turns, the radian-as-unit debate, and physics done by counting phase

(Research notes written by Claude, 2026-10-25, for the report writer. Every claim carries its source; unverified items are in Gaps.)

## Who started the tau (turn) movement, what was argued, what got adopted, and what the critics said?

### Takeaway
Bob Palais's "π Is Wrong!" (Mathematical Intelligencer, 2001) argued that the circle constant should be one full turn, and Michael Hartl's "Tau Manifesto" (Tau Day, June 28, 2010) named it τ = 2π and popularized it. Since then τ has shipped as a named constant in mainstream languages (Python 3.6, Rust 1.47, .NET, Unity), and the "turn" itself has had a long life as a unit in standards, calculators, and engineering, independent of the τ campaign. The main pushback (Cavers's "Pi Manifesto") rests on diameter being easier to measure, A = πr² being tidier, and π-flavored formulas in statistics; the second-order criticism is symbol clash (τ for torque, time constant, torsion).

### Cited Findings
- Palais, "π is wrong!", *The Mathematical Intelligencer* 23(3), 7–8 (2001). He proposed a symbol for 2π that he called "one turn" (originally a three-legged π), arguing 90° is naturally a quarter turn rather than π/2, that π obscures the benefits of radian measure, and comparing the choice of π to defining the speed of light as half its value. — [Palais, Univ. of Utah page](https://www.math.utah.edu/~palais/pi.html)
- Palais later (c. March 2011) endorsed the letter τ, citing Peter Harremoës, Fred Hoyle's 1962 astronomy book, H. Laurent's 1889 usage, and Hartl's manifesto; he judged that clashes with τ for torque, time constants, shear stress, and Frenet–Serret torsion can be handled by context. — [Palais, Univ. of Utah page](https://www.math.utah.edu/~palais/pi.html)
- Hartl's "The Tau Manifesto" was first published on Tau Day 2010 (June 28) and updated Tau Day 2023. Arguments: radius, not diameter, defines a circle; an angle is θ = fτ with f the fraction of a turn (quarter turn = τ/4), so nothing special needs memorizing; e^{iτ} = 1 says "one full turn returns you to the start"; A = ½τr² matches the quadratic forms ½gt², ½kx², ½mv². It credits Harremoës, John Fisher, and Joseph Lindenberg as earlier τ proposers and includes a rebuttal of the Pi Manifesto (the Gaussian's 2π, Γ(½), and so on). — [Hartl, The Tau Manifesto (PDF, tauday.com)](https://tauday.com/pdf)
- Criticism: Michael Cavers's "The Pi Manifesto" (shortly after Hartl's) called the Tau Manifesto selectively biased: π is historically C/d and the diameter is easier to measure; A = πr² is simpler; probability and statistics formulas would pick up factors of ½. Compromise proposals include a new angle unit ("darians", from "The Proper Pi Manifesto") and xkcd's joke "pau" = 1.5π. The article's conclusion: the math doesn't change, but notation shapes intuition, and two competing constants would confuse. — [Scientific American, "Why some mathematicians think we should abandon pi"](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-some-mathematicians-think-we-should-abandon-pi/)
- Python: PEP 628 "Add math.tau" (author Alyssa Coghlan, created June 28, 2011), status Final, shipped in Python 3.6 (2016). Rationale: a circle is defined by its center and radius; quarter circle = tau/4 rather than pi/2. — [PEP 628](https://peps.python.org/pep-0628)
- Rust: `std::f64::consts::TAU`, "The full circle constant (τ). Equal to 2π.", stable since Rust 1.47.0. — [Rust docs](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/f64/consts/constant.TAU.html)
- .NET: `Math.Tau` / `MathF.Tau` (= 2π, "a full rotation in radians") approved by API review ("Approved as Tau"); objections recorded that "tau" has several meanings. — [dotnet/runtime issue #24678](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/issues/24678)
- Unity's Mathematics package defines `math.TAU` and `math.TAU_DBL`. — [Unity Mathematics docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.mathematics@1.3/api/Unity.Mathematics.math.TAU_DBL.html)
- The turn as a unit predates τ and lives on separately: ISO 80000-3:2006 gave "revolution" (symbol r) as a special name for the unit one, "widely used in specifications on rotating machines"; ISO 80000-3:2019 defines *rotation* (symbol N) as the dimensionless number of revolutions; DIN 1315 (1974) proposed "pla" (*plenus angulus*, full angle); DIN 1301-1 (2010) lists the *Vollwinkel*, legal in the EU though not SI; IET symbols use "rev"/"cyc"; Fred Hoyle (1962) used centiturn/milliturn/microturn; milliturn scales appeared on artillery and satellite-tracking instruments (1965, 1975); percentage protractors date to Croxton (1922); computing uses binary fractions of a turn ("binary radians", brad = 1/256 turn). HP calculators support a "tr" angle unit (HP 39gII 2011, HP Prime 2013, later firmware for others). — [Wikipedia, "Turn (angle)", with its cited standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_(angle))

### Inferences
- Two distinct ideas travel together and should be kept apart: (1) τ as a *number* (the circle constant, still meaning "radians in a turn"), which is what the languages adopted; and (2) the *turn as a unit* of angle, which engineers, standards bodies, and calculator makers adopted without caring about τ. A toolkit that writes angles in turns is closer to (2); the τ movement only partly supplies it, since Hartl's θ = fτ still converts back to radians.
- The τ adopters fixed a programming convenience (no `2*pi` everywhere) and left the unit question untouched: `math.tau` is just a float, and Pint-style unit libraries still treat the radian as dimensionless (see the next section).

### Gaps
- No source found giving counts of textbooks or school curricula that teach with τ; the Scientific American piece says only that "some teachers and students" use it.
- The .NET version that first shipped `Math.Tau` isn't stated in the issue thread (likely .NET 5, unverified).
- Cavers's original "Pi Manifesto" page wasn't retrieved directly (Hartl says it was later taken down); its arguments are reported second-hand above.

## Is the radian a real unit? Its SI status, the proposals to make angle a base dimension, the problems claimed, and where the CCU stands as of 2024–2026

### Takeaway
The radian and steradian were "supplementary units" until the 20th CGPM (1995) folded them into dimensionless derived units (rad = m/m = 1), following a 1980 CIPM interpretation. A long line of authors (Brinsmade 1936, Romain 1962, Eder 1982, Torrens 1986, Brownstein 1997, Mohr & Phillips 2015, Quincey and colleagues 2016–2021, Leonard 2021, Mohr et al. 2022, Kalinin 2019/2025) argues that angle deserves its own dimension, citing Hz-vs-rad/s errors of 2π, torque sharing joules with energy, and ħ's ambiguity. Mathematicians (Grötschel, Hanche-Olsen, Holden) push back that angle measure is a pure number. The CCU found no consensus in 2021, formed a task group that ran 2021–May 2024, and the outcome was wording changes in SI Brochure v3.01 (August 2024) that explicitly did *not* change the status of angle; nothing found shows a status change through October 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Status in the SI**
- 20th CGPM (1995), Resolution 8: approving the CIPM's 1980 interpretation, decides "to interpret the supplementary units in the SI, namely the radian and the steradian, as dimensionless derived units, the names and symbols of which may, but need not, be used in expressions for other SI derived units, as is convenient" and "to eliminate the class of supplementary units as a separate class in the SI." — [BIPM, CGPM 1995 Resolution 8](https://www.bipm.org/en/committees/cg/cgpm/20-1995/resolution-8)
- The 1980 CIPM decision (CIPM Recommendation 1, 1980, "SI supplementary units (radian and steradian)") is the origin of the dimensionless interpretation. — [BIPM SI Brochure, Appendix 1 index](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure/annex-1/derived-units); per Kalinin, the CCU backed the ISO TC 12 position, the SI Brochure has treated rad and sr as dimensionless derived units since 1981, and the dispute continues at ISO TC 12, CCU, and CIPM, now extended to angular frequency and phase angle — [Kalinin, "On the dual nature of a plane angle," arXiv:2605.06586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06586)
- Quincey (2021) says the 1980 proposal to give angle base-unit status "was turned down" by the CCU "with a notable lack of full agreement among the members." — [Quincey, "Angles in the SI: a detailed proposal for solving the problem," Metrologia 2021, arXiv:2108.05704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05704)
- The SI Brochure's own warning (as quoted in Pint's docs): Hz and rad/s are both reciprocal seconds, "it is especially important to carefully distinguish frequencies from angular frequencies," "by definition their numerical values differ by a factor of 2π," and "Ignoring this fact may cause an error of 2π"; always give explicit units of Hz or rad/s. Only frequency takes the name hertz (SI Brochure §2.2.2); e.g., 60 rpm = 2π rad/s angular velocity = 1 Hz rotational frequency. — [Pint docs, "Angles and Angular Frequency"](https://pint.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/angular_frequency.html); [Wikipedia, "Hertz"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz)
- The name hertz was set by the IEC in 1935 and adopted by the CGPM in 1960, replacing "cycles per second." — [Wikipedia, "Hertz"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz)
- Software consequence: Pint treats angle as dimensionless, so `1 rad` has empty dimensionality (it can't check that something is an angle), and `60 rpm` converts to about 6.28 Hz rather than 1 Hz, because revolutions are converted through radians. — [Pint docs](https://pint.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/angular_frequency.html)

**The proposals and the problems they name (chronological)**
- Brinsmade, "Plane and Solid Angles; their Pedagogic Value When Introduced Explicitly," *Am. J. Phys.* 4, 175–179 (1936); Romain, "Angle as a Fourth Fundamental Quantity," *J. Res. NBS* 66B, 97–100 (1962); Eder, "A Viewpoint on the Quantity 'Plane Angle'," *Metrologia* 18, 1–12 (1982); Torrens, "On angles and angular quantities," *Metrologia* 22, 1–7 (1986); Freeman, "SI Units of Frequency, Angular Velocity, Planck's Constant and ħ," *Metrologia* 23, 221–222 (1986) (proposed cycle per second for frequency); Brownstein, "Angles—Let's treat them squarely," *Am. J. Phys.* 65(7), 605–614 (1997); Foster, *Metrologia* 47, R41–R51 (2010). — [Quincey & Brown, "Implications of adopting plane angle as a base quantity in the SI," Metrologia 53, 998–1002 (2016), reference list, arXiv:1604.02373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02373)
- Quincey & Brown (2016): with angle as a base quantity, energy = torque × angle (parallel to force × distance), so torque gains rad⁻¹; frequency vs. angular velocity become distinguishable (a cycle could be a base unit, cy/s, or a cycle treated as a revolution-sized angle, credited to Ian Mills); they prefer keeping frequency as s⁻¹ with E = hf, and ħ either carries kg m² s⁻¹ rad⁻¹ or the equation carries an explicit factor η (E = ηħω), with ħ = ηh/2π; Torrens's η notation is adopted. — [Quincey & Brown 2016, arXiv:1604.02373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02373)
- Mohr & Phillips, "Dimensionless units in the SI," *Metrologia* 52 (2015): the SI should be coherent, but replacing different dimensionless units (radians, steradians, counts of decays or molecules) by a single unit "one" produces incoherence; "the radian is the appropriate coherent unit for angles and … hertz is not a coherent unit in the SI." — [NIST publication page](https://www.nist.gov/publications/dimensionless-units-si). Page numbers conflict: NIST lists 52, 617–618; Quincey 2021's reference list gives 52, 40–47 — [arXiv:2108.05704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05704). (The 40–47 paper may be the main article and 617–618 a later reply; unresolved.)
- Quincey's comment (2015) argued Mohr & Phillips's fixes "bring new problems of their own" and proposed instead: clarify the angle text in the Brochure; reclassify the hertz as a non-SI unit accepted for use with the SI, restricted to revolutions or cycles per second; present counts as pure numbers with a description of what's counted. — [Quincey, "Comment on 'Dimensionless units in the SI'," arXiv:1505.07230](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.07230)
- Mohr & Phillips, "A proposal to classify the radian as a base unit in the SI" (arXiv 2016): plane angle and phase get their own dimension; rad/s becomes the coherent unit for angular frequency; Hz is no longer coherent; the steradian becomes rad²; dropping "rad" in current practice has caused errors. — [arXiv:1604.06774](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06774)
- Quincey, "Natural units in physics, and the curious case of the radian," *Physics Education* 51(6), 065012 (2016): the SI treats the radian as a natural unit, unusually, blurring angle-related quantities; the paper shows the SI without that exception and doesn't propose changing the SI, aiming at clarity, software, and teaching. — [NPL eprint 7258](https://eprintspublications.npl.co.uk/7258/)
- Quincey, Mohr & Phillips, "Angles are inherently neither length ratios nor dimensionless," *Metrologia* 56, 043001 (2019): s = rθ holds only with θ in radians and angle dimensionless; the unit-independent form is s = rθ/θ_N; measuring angle by an arc ratio is not defining it (as mass is measured but not defined by force ratios); a paper-folding protractor measures angle without length; rad ≠ m/m any more than kg/kg is special; options are a base quantity, a dimensional derived quantity, or θ_N = 1 by explicit convention. — [arXiv:1909.08389](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08389)
- Leonard, "Proposal for the dimensionally consistent treatment of angle and solid angle by the International System of Units," *Metrologia* 58, 052001 (2021). — [cited in Quincey 2021, arXiv:2108.05704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05704)
- Quincey (2021) lists the problems with the dimensionless radian: frequency and angular frequency both s⁻¹ ("anomalies and disappearing factors of 2π"); torque, angular momentum, and moment of inertia units (N m, J s, kg m²) omit the radian, which is optional elsewhere; dimensional analysis can't be applied fully to equations with angles; setting the angle constant to 1 hides it so equations look unit-invariant when they aren't; phase treated as dimensionless clashes with ωt being an angle; ħ used for both action and angular momentum; software ambiguity. Proposal: torque's unit becomes J rad⁻¹, so "torque × angle = energy," while allowing an explicitly stated convention "1 rad = 1" inside equations. — [arXiv:2108.05704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05704)
- Mohr, Shirley, Phillips & Trott, "On the dimension of angles and their units" (2022, arXiv:2203.12392): give angles true dimension, use "complete" trigonometric and transcendental functions, and extract a constant of angular dimension (value one radian) when differentiating by angle, fixing dimensional imbalances. — [Dozenal Society review of the paper](https://dozenal.org/article-volan-review-mohr-et-al-1206.html)
- Kalinin, "On the dual nature of a plane angle" (arXiv 2026, latest refs 2025): angle as measured/communicated is a dimensional base quantity with the radian as base unit, while in theoretical equations angles enter as a dimensionless combination; he calls the Brochure's θ = s/r a recipe for the numerical value, not a definition. — [arXiv:2605.06586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06586)
- Counter-position: Grötschel, Hanche-Olsen & Holden (comment dated Sept 1, 2021, on Quincey/Mohr/Phillips 2019): angle measure is mathematical, arc/radius is a pure number, "radian" is an alias for one and can't be a base unit; Euclidean geometry has privileged angles (the right angle) but no privileged length, so lengths need a unit and angles don't; conservation laws don't depend on unit choice. They also object to the Brochure's rad = m/m but want rad kept as an alias for 1. — [Grötschel et al. comment (PDF)](https://www.zib.de/groetschel/pubnew/geometry-opinion-comment.pdf)

### Inferences
- As of October 2026, the official position is unchanged since 1995: the radian is a dimensionless derived unit that "may, but need not" be written. The only movement is editorial (v3.01) plus encouragement to write units explicitly. The institutional message ("stability and consistency") suggests no status change before a 10th edition around 2030 at the earliest.
- A toolkit that treats the radian (a "stroke") as a real unit is squarely in the Brownstein / Quincey / Mohr–Phillips / Leonard camp. Their concrete payoffs line up with what a beginner toolkit wants: torque in J/rad (energy = torque × angle), angular frequency distinct from Hz, and the turn (cycle, revolution) as a legitimate angle unit (Mills's suggestion; Quincey's "Hz restricted to cycles per second").
- The NIST/IUPAP vs. PTB/NMIJ split shows the dimensional-angle view is respectable but a minority among national metrology institutes; the strongest opposition comes from mathematicians who see angle measure as a pure number.

## Which fields already do physics by accumulating phase or counting turns, in what units, and what each fixed for its own needs?

### Takeaway
Counting whole cycles plus a measured fraction is everyday practice in GNSS carrier-phase positioning (integer ambiguity N, cycle slips, L1 wavelength ≈ 19 cm), interferometric length metrology (the 1960–1983 meter was literally 1 650 763.73 wavelengths), InSAR (one color fringe ≈ 2.8 cm of ground motion for Sentinel-1), PLLs/phasemeters (phase unwrapping beyond ±π), rotary encoders (counts per revolution), and semiclassical quantum mechanics (Bohr–Sommerfeld ∮p dq = (n + c)h, where c counts quarter-cycle phase losses at turning points). Feynman's "stopwatch" arrows and the Aharonov–Bohm and Berry phases are also stated as fractions of a cycle. Each field built its own count-plus-fraction bookkeeping and kept trigonometry inside instruments rather than in the reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- **GNSS carrier phase:** measured carrier phase = range + (ambiguity × wavelength) + errors, Φ = r + Nλ + errors, with N the whole number of carrier cycles between satellite and receiver at lock-on. The receiver measures only the fractional cycle at lock-on, then tracks phase continuously; N stays constant "unless any cycle slip occurs," after which it takes "a new constant integer value." L1 wavelength ≈ 19 cm, λ₁ = c/f₁. — [NPTEL (IIT) GPS module, carrier phase](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/105104100/lectureB_12/B_12_6carrier.htm)
- **Interferometric length:** the 11th CGPM (1960) defined the meter as "the length equal to 1 650 763,73 wavelengths in vacuum of the radiation corresponding to the transition between the levels 2p10 and 5d5 of the krypton 86 atom"; abrogated in 1983. — [BIPM, CGPM 1960 Resolution 6](https://www.bipm.org/committees/cg/cgpm/11-1960/resolution-6)
- **InSAR (radar geodesy):** "Each fringe (which is associated to a colour cycle) corresponds to approximately 2.8 cm of displacement" along line of sight (Sentinel-1 interferogram, Aug. 20 and 26, 2016, Italy earthquake). — [Copernicus](https://www.copernicus.eu/en/node/5973)
- **Phase meters / PLLs / unwrapping:** a lock-in amplifier's arctangent phase detector is "limited to one cycle of dynamic range" ([-π, π]) and jumps at the boundary; a PLL-based phasemeter integrates phase changes on its internal oscillator and so is "immune to the phase changes caused by the arctangent's effective range limitations"; unwrapped phase "can be inaccurate if the signal is obscured by the noise floor." — [Liquid Instruments knowledge base](https://liquidinstruments.helpjuice.com/lock-in-amplifier2/can-moku-perform-phase-unwrapping-demodulation). The same instrument reports phase in radians (precision "1 nrad"), with selectable output wrap of off, ±π, ±2π, or ±4π, via a "digitally implemented phase-locked loop." — [Moku:Lab Phasemeter datasheet (PDF)](https://assets.testequity.com/te1/Documents/pdf/liquid_instruments/Liquid_Instruments_Datasheet-MokuLab-Phasemeter.pdf)
- **Semiclassical quantization (WKB / Bohr–Sommerfeld):** ∮p dx = (n + c)h; each smooth classical turning point contributes a π/4 phase (a quarter of a half-cycle; "the fourth root of (−1)") from the JWKB connection formulas, giving c = ½ for a smooth well, c = ¾ with one hard wall, c = 1 with two; c is the Maslov index. — [Rau, "Dimensions, nodes and phases in quantum numbers," arXiv:0906.4871](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.4871)
- **Aharonov–Bohm (measured as fringe fraction):** Tonomura's 1986 Hitachi experiment (toroidal magnet sealed in superconducting niobium, 5 K) found that "Interference fringes are displaced with just half a fringe of spacing inside and outside of the doughnut," i.e., a half-cycle phase difference. — [Hitachi R&D, Aharonov–Bohm effect](https://www-hitachi-com.itdweb.ext.hitachi.co.jp/rd/research/materials/quantum/aharonov-bohm/index.html)
- **Berry (geometric) phase:** M. V. Berry, "Quantal Phase Factors Accompanying Adiabatic Changes," *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A* 392, 45ff (1984), doi:10.1098/rspa.1984.0023. — [PDF copy](https://www.ifsc.usp.br/~hoyos/courses/2013/SFI5707/Berry.pdf)
- **Feynman's arrows taught as counted rotations:** Edwin F. Taylor's "OneParticle" photon exercises (July 2000), built on Feynman's QED picture, have a "fictional quantum stopwatch [that] meters the rotation of the little arrow for that path," with an on-screen count of rotations (an exercise asks for a point where "the number of rotations is 10.5"). — [Taylor, One-Photon Exercises (PDF)](https://www.eftaylor.com/software/OnePhotonExercises.pdf)
- **Rotating machinery, units on the turn:** ISO 80000-3 defines rotation N as the number of revolutions; "revolution" was used "in specifications on rotating machines"; frequency units such as rpm and cycles per second count turns directly. — [Wikipedia, "Turn (angle)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_(angle)). Pint's docs show the tooling cost: rpm → Hz conversions come out 2π too large because the library routes revolutions through dimensionless radians. — [Pint docs](https://pint.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/angular_frequency.html)

### Inferences
- The shared pattern is "integer count + measured fraction of a cycle," with the integer recovered by continuous tracking (PLL, carrier tracking, fringe counting) and lost in a "cycle slip." This is the turn-and-fraction bookkeeping a beginner toolkit would teach, and it's how these professionals actually work.
- Units vary by field, and each picked what kept its numbers whole: GNSS and InSAR speak in cycles/fringes and convert with λ; interferometry speaks in wavelengths or fringes; instruments like the Moku report radians; semiclassical physics writes ∮p dq in units of h (whole cycles), with the Maslov correction counted in quarter-cycles. Where a field counts, it counts turns; radians appear mostly where calculus is being done.
- What these fields left out: none of them made the cycle a unit inside a coherent unit system; each keeps a private conversion (λ, h vs ħ, 2π) at the boundary. That's the gap the metrology debate in the previous section is about.

## Is there physics education research or proposals on teaching angle as turns or with the radian as a unit, and evidence that 2π confuses students?

### Takeaway
Proposals to teach angle explicitly as a dimensional quantity go back to Brinsmade (Am. J. Phys., 1936) and Brownstein (Am. J. Phys., 1997), with recent teaching-oriented treatments by Quincey (Physics Education, 2016) and Redish (2020 preprint). The clearest empirical evidence is a 2025 multinational study: only about a quarter of 769 students knew the standard trigonometric derivatives hold only in radians. No study was found that directly tests teaching in turns or measures confusion from the 2π factor specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Brinsmade, "Plane and Solid Angles; their Pedagogic Value When Introduced Explicitly," *Am. J. Phys.* 4, 175–179 (1936), the earliest pedagogical argument found for writing angle units explicitly. — [cited in Quincey & Brown 2016, arXiv:1604.02373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02373)
- Brownstein, "Angles—Let's treat them squarely," *Am. J. Phys.* 65(7), 605–614 (1997). — [cited in arXiv:1604.02373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02373)
- Quincey (2016, *Physics Education*) presents the SI-without-the-radian-exception explicitly to clarify how angles relate to physics for teaching and software. — [NPL eprint 7258](https://eprintspublications.npl.co.uk/7258/)
- Redish, "Using math in physics — 1. Dimensional analysis" (preprint, 2020): treating radians as dimensionless because θ = s/r "misses the critical issue"; scale choice for angle is free (degrees, gradians), so "we usually don't bother to assign a dimensionality to an angle (but we probably should)"; he notes this differs from the AAPT recommendation, suggests sticking with radians for students as workable, and observes that students often make mistakes with angular variables. — [arXiv:2011.12760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12760)
- Ben-Zion, Liu, Wang, Rajak & Finkelstein, "Global Blind Spot in Understanding Trigonometric Derivatives: A Multinational Analysis" (arXiv, Mar. 4, 2025): of 769 students (Israel 272, USA 164, China 186, India 147), only 26.3% correctly said the standard trig derivatives hold only in radians; 70.7% said both radians and degrees work, 3% degrees only. In a harmonic-oscillator context, 59.0% chose radians, 34.7% both, 6.2% degrees. — [arXiv:2503.01525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01525)
- Hartl's manifesto makes the pedagogical claim (not empirically tested) that writing θ = fτ, with f the fraction of a turn, removes the need to memorize special angles. — [Tau Manifesto (PDF)](https://tauday.com/pdf)
- A Brazilian physics-teaching paper addresses "Plane Angles: why must the radian have a measure equal to the unity?" (*Caderno Brasileiro de Ensino de Física* 26(1), 136, 2009). — [UFSC journal page](https://periodicos.ufsc.br/index.php/fisica/article/view/2175-7941.2009v26n1p136)

### Inferences
- The Ben-Zion et al. result is direct evidence that the "invisible radian" hurts students: when the unit isn't written, students can't tell that d/dθ sin θ = cos θ depends on the unit. A toolkit that writes the stroke (radian) explicitly and makes turns the default addresses exactly that blind spot, and Mohr et al.'s 2022 "constant of angular dimension that appears on differentiation" is the formal version of the same fix.
- Educators who favor dimensional angle (Redish) still advise radians-as-1 for students for pragmatic reasons, so a turns-first beginner toolkit would be ahead of current PER practice, not following it.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found that compares teaching angle in turns (or with τ) against radians/π, and no study isolating confusion caused by factors of 2π (as opposed to unit confusion generally).
- Brownstein's 1997 article content wasn't retrieved; only its citation is confirmed.
- The AAPT recommendation Redish mentions wasn't located or quoted.
- The publication venue for Ben-Zion et al. (2025) beyond arXiv wasn't confirmed.
