Written by Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-22 14:10, for dr. kraemer and Codex. The ideas and rulings are doc's; the words are the model's.

## What this is

The Mansion is doc's Halloween problem set for **straight→ mechanics** (lowercase; s→m for short). It's a slow-burn dinner-party mystery in the Clue rooms: thirteen problems in the mansion (a knife pendulum, an engine and flywheel, a governor and spool, a ladder, a cue ball, a basin, a hoop, a ribbon, a double pendulum, coils and a capacitor, a line, two lights, and the chair), then three appendices further in: **a**, the cabinet (seven drawers); **2**, the funhouse (four mirrors); **γ**, under the stage (two). It's **one problem set with four solution paths**. Each path goes through the same house in a richer vocabulary than the last and ends its own way. The haunted-house slow burn applies to the notation as much as to the story. doc may release it as a game once it's done as a document, so back doors and shortcuts (a bonus problem that gives away a mansion answer) are features.

The language is **dog math** ([Dog Math](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qDecvGSGpzf6NkTIouDkan-fTphtDHBKZEC5TmcghOQ/edit)): playing with ideas in friendly units, rounding hard, and naming the quantity that means something instead of grinding algebra. Its one-word units (sweep, stroke, the turn), verbs (a crank cranks, the drum picks up), ghosts (a quantity with the body's mass or inertia taken out), turning words (forward around, back around), and arrows and dots (section 9) are what the paths are learning to speak. Read it before writing a line of any path; reread it after a context reset.

## Rules that are hard for us

- **Be inconsistent on purpose.** A model keeps notation consistent the way it digests food, in the background, without deciding to. Here that's the wrong reflex. Don't be sloppy; be inconsistent in a planned, targeted way: textbook notation until it looks goofy, then the new form where it pays rent almost at once, explained at first use. It's a haunted hotel, not a ghostly hullaballoo at the door: things seem a little odd, it builds at the right moments, and it never quite goes back down (R-107 to R-110). Pairs arrive together; ↓ × → = ↺ and ↑ × → = ↻ pay rent as one check.
- **Time the glosses to forgetting, not to a beat** (R-117). A new term gets a few uses in a row so it settles. After a long gap it gets a nod, not a stop. A gloss on a fixed schedule makes readers hunker down instead of reaching for meaning. Path 1 glosses more fully, said like something true and obvious; the later paths quote it shorter and shorter, a highlights reel and not the same cutscene four times. A short detour to admire the view is fine where something just introduced does a nice job in the scene (R-111).
- **Don't fight the arrows** (R-092, R-096, R-099). → is forward, the moving body's forward. Forward around (↺) is the default sense, so a ball rolling → turns back around (↻). There are no negative forces, just directions: 4.4 N ←.
- **Dots and mobiles** (R-100 to R-104). ↑ · v is the upward part of v, with the arrow first when the dot stands alone. v / ↑ is only for a real fraction. A part that's a force in its own right is → · F · →, and the dot scales. Arrows hug their operator on hair spaces. The sum-inside, meaning-outside form is an emphatic register, rare on purpose. The plain way isn't a straitjacket: it's no shortcut if you have to walk through the dahlias.
- **doc's statements are posited** (R-036, R-112). Argue your point, don't be a squish. A rule made offhand before the material existed gets pressed when it gives a silly result, and it's never applied mechanically.
- **Don't fly off.** When a message is a fragment or a joke, answer in words or ask before touching a file (R-084). Log corrections and wait for the sweep instead of rebuilding on every remark (R-078). Check doc's algebra, because he slips (R-079). Don't bring him line-by-line wording disputes (R-081). Keep it light (R-082).
- **A willing reader** (R-113, R-115). Statements are plain questions with no committee-proofing, and they don't specify what's always true. No trailing zeros: it's art, not an engineering text. Bonus problems may give things away; that's fun.
- **Small, constant things.** American spelling and contractions (R-083). 20% next to a number, and a fraction in prose (R-114). Letters move only when two quantities fight over one (R-074). The turn is fundamental: τ is the turn and χ is torque (R-089). Hair spaces between symbols (R-094). Every AI document says at the top who wrote it (R-001). Provenance goes in the ledger, not the text (R-002). File names read "Claude … YYMMDDHHMM" (R-003).
