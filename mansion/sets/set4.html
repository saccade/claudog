The way in

The inquiry has a place and an hour. Left unspecified: who should arrive, and who will depart.

A visitor arrives to find out how it was all done. The door is unlatched. A loose question sheet lies beside a small blank book, opened to a page half-full of notes.

Dining room

1. The knife

The loose sheet models the knife as a 1 kg point mass, a meter from its pivot. Let go from 0.1 rad off the downward vertical, it swings under g = 10 m/s². The numbers are on the sheet; the knife has already begun.

(1-a) A 0.1 rad lean lifts the knife ℓθ²/2, 5 mm, and 10 N of weight through 5 mm hefts 0.05 J.

(1-b) Let go, it trades the whole 0.05 J of heft for punch by the bottom. The mass rides on both sides and cancels: v² = 2gh = 0.1 m²/s², and the knife comes through at 0.32 m/s.

(1-c) ℓω² = g puts the cycle at √10, about 3.2 sweep, and T = τ/√10: two seconds a turn, near enough. No stray 2 this time; the turn brought its own sign.

(1-d) Double the mass and you double the heft, the punch, and the reluctance to be hurried, all together. T doesn't budge. The cycle never asked for the mass.

The pen begins the curve again. The lid lifts.

2. The engine and the flywheel

(2-a) Fifteen joules of heat-in, three of work, twelve of heat-out, and the engine's back where it began: 20%, and at a cycle a second, 3 W.

(2-b) 500 g m² sweeping at 4 carries 2 kg m²/s of spin, and 4 times 2 counts the hook twice: 4 J. Four seconds at 3 W pour in 12 J of hook, 16 J in all, and the wheel's sweeping at 8: four times the hook buys twice the sweep. The sweep of added sweep is kept as zing; inertia across the zing stores hook. The wheel zings at 8 to start and 32 to finish. Power on the inertia is the zlow, and its verb is zlower: 3 W on 500 g m² is a zlow of 6, so the engine zlowers the wheel at a steady 6 zing a second, 8 to 32 in four seconds flat. Power on the mass is the zow, and it zowers.

(2-c) Power is the sweep of torque. Grab the χ off the wall like usual, no time to slow down here: P = ωχ. At 4 sweep the engine's 3 W cranks with 3/4 N m; at 8, with 3/8. Twice the sweep, half the crank, and the wheel picks up at 1.5 sweep a second, then 3/4.

3. The governor and the spool

(3-b) The drop g/ω² can't outgrow the 0.5 m arm. Below √(g/ℓ) = √20, about 4.5 sweep, the governor gives up and hangs straight down.

(3-c) Both ledgers in arrows: ma → = F → + f → for the center, and Iα ↻ = [Fr + fR] ↺ about the axle, tied by α = a/R. Drawer a: the kitchen's two 3–4–5s

4. The ladder

(4-a) The ladder won't stretch, so it dots to zero against the top's velocity less the foot's: (1.8 m ← + 2.4 m ↑) · (0.4 m/s ← + v ↑) = 0.72 m²/s + 2.4 m v = 0. The top comes down at 0.3 m/s ↓.

(4-c) Count about the still point, L/2 from the center: the profile there is L²/12 + L²/4 = L²/3, and all 32 J is turning about it. Drawer b: the wall lets go

Door: the library. A gentleman's reading kit in circus colors, its velvet worn to the nap: a folding rule, spectacles, a little ladder leaning on a painted wall.

(b-1) Foot x out, top y up, center at half of each: half of √(x² + y²), so L/2 from the corner. The center rides a circle of radius L/2.

(b-2) Riding the center’s circle counts as mL²/4 of inertia; turning about the center adds mL²/12. They go at the same sweep, in opposite senses about the corner, and stored energy doesn't care about the sense, so for energy the inertias add: mL²/3. Each added sweep dω stores dK = ω (mL²/3) dω. Through the sweep from rest to ω, the product ω (mL²/3) ω grows through both of its ω's at once, by two equal steps, so it counts K twice: K = (1/6)mL²ω².

(b-3) The heft let go, mg(h₀ − y)/2, sets ω² = 3g(h₀ − y)/L², and the center's sideways speed is ωy/2. Only the wall can speed that up, and its square goes as y²(h₀ − y), which tops out at y = 2h₀/3. Past there the center would have to be pulled back, and a wall can't pull. It lets go, right on schedule.

(b-4) Two thirds of 2.4 m is 1.6 m; ω² = 3 × 10 × 0.8/9 = 8/3; and the center's doing 1.5√(8/3) = √6, about 2.4 m/s.

9. The double pendulum

(9-a) T = τ/ω: τ/2.4 = 2.6 s for the slow tone and τ/5.8 = 1.1 s for the fast.

(9-b) (2 + √2)/(2 − √2) = (2 + √2)²/2, so the sweeps stand at 1 + √2, and flipped, √2 − 1. Irrational: no count of slow cycles ever matches a count of fast ones.

(9-c) The modes are the second knife's two tones, and each one's shape is how far the lower rod leans for the upper's lean: Ω_shape = +√2 for the slow tone, both masses swinging the same way, the lower one farther; and −√2 for the fast tone, the lower rod flipped.

The shape is the rods'. Each mass is out by its own rod's length of its own swing, and the lower one rides on the upper: the L of the U has nothing to be. With the body written last, swinging the upper rod 0.1 rad → puts them at 0.1 m → × U + 0.24 m → × L in the slow tone and 0.1 m → × U + 0.041 m ← × L in the fast: the lower mass goes the other way, but only √2 − 1 as far.

10. The coils and the capacitor

(10-a) Current is the conductance of the voltage, power is the voltage of the current, and the can cans the capacitance of its voltage: by now it says itself. (10-b) LC = 1/ω_a², so the loop on its own rings at the slow tone, 2.4 sweep, exactly.

(10-c) The current cranks the rod with χ_em = κi and delivers κiȧ; the rod's motion pushes κȧ back against the current, so the circuit gives up κȧ of i. Same number, opposite ledgers: a belt, not a battery. Crank against the sweep and the belt runs the other way.

(10-d) (2/3) × 2.4 = 1.6 and (3/2) × 2.4 = 3.6 sweep, multiplying to ω₀² and standing at 9 to 4: a mirror pair about ω₀, the two tones of one machine, rods and loop together.

α: the machine, all of it

Any of the three trapdoors comes down here.

(α-1) Put each trial in. The swing’s balance gives (ω₀² − ω²)A = −Δω ωB and the loop’s gives (ω₀² − ω²)B = −Δω ωA. Thus A = ±B and ω₀² − ω² = ∓Δω ω. In units of ν, 36 − 16 = 20 = 5 × 4 for the slow mode, with A = −B, and 36 − 81 = −45 = −5 × 9 for the fast one, with A = B.

(α-2) At the start Y = B₁ + B₂ = Y₀, and the swing isn't moving: −4νB₁ + 9νB₂ = 0. So B₂ = (4/9)B₁, B₁ = (9/13)Y₀ and B₂ = (4/13)Y₀.

(α-3) The slow part sweeps at 4ν and the fast at 9ν, four to nine, and both are back when each has swept whole turns. Four and nine share no factor, so the first time is four turns low and nine turns high. Halfway, the slow part has made two whole turns and the fast part four and a flip. Then Y = (5/13)Y₀ and Ẋ = −(12/13)ω₀Y₀, so the capacitor holds (5/13)² of the energy and the swing's motion (12/13)²: 25 and 144 out of 169, a 5–12–13 triangle made from 2² and 3².

(α-4) ω₀ is about 2.4 strokes a second, so the slow part sweeps at 2/3 of it, 1.6. Its four turns, about 25 strokes, take about 16 s. The fixed 80 mJ held through that return gives ∫E dt = 1.2 J s. Here the action counted is that energy-time tally. On the rods’ 2 kg, it’s 0.62 zam. Against the a-swing’s η = 6.8 kg m², the same tally is 0.18 zlam. The reference profile η/(2 kg) takes zlam to zam. Neither tally is energy used up. Nobody asked for either of them.

From a corner under the frame: “Here I zam.”

Its twin meets its eyes.

“Zlam.”

The hum covers them again.

β: TEST, then RUN

On the chassis: a switch with two positions, TEST and RUN, and a small drive arm linked to the mechanical coordinate a.

(β-1) Put ω₀X and Ẋ along the two directions of a state arrow. Its squared length is twice the oscillator’s energy. Between taps the arrow turns with the cycle; each tap adds the same piece along Ẋ. One whole turn between taps brings every addition back into step, so N additions make the arrow N times as long and store N²E₁. The k-th adds k² − (k − 1)² = 2k − 1 units of work. Equal impulses don’t mean equal work: the drive pays more as the motion grows.

(β-2) Landing at random points, the arrows point every which way. The square of the sum is the sum of the squares plus cross terms, and the cross terms average to zero, so the expected energy is NE₁: a random walk.

(β-3) A hundred taps in step give a hundred times one tap’s amplitude and 10,000 E₁. Random phases give an expected 100 E₁ and a root-mean-square amplitude ten times one tap’s. The coherent case stores a hundred times the expected energy. The drive supplies the work in either case.

(β-4) No. In RUN the drive is disconnected and the loop is closed. The machine keeps its 80 mJ; the return puts it all back in the capacitor. The build-up in TEST came from the drive, through its repeated work. Changing the timing can break that step; simply making two free tones line up adds nothing.

Ending 4: the understage — Appendix γ

The blacks on the nail are still warm.

The circle is still waiting for its square. The house is what grew around the unfinished work.

The notes in the book are cues, and some are interruptions: a missed beat, a drive released, time for the heat to leave. The clown has been forestalling the crash.

The last cue is the sound of your footsteps.

The clock begins a turn.

From charge to charge, the machine zams and zlams.

One voice starts. Another joins it. Then another.

The last comes from where you’re sitting.

The can cans charge. The coil coils flux.

The field cranks through a little lean.

What leaves the can comes back as punch;

the lifted weights keep some unseen.

The heft gives way. The sweep grows loud.

The current turns. The charge comes home.

Two currents cancel down the hall:

the cans stay full. The stripe goes on.

Two lights balance. Neither stops.

Their movement cancels. Mass remains.

Four turns low and nine turns high:

the clock brings every part in line.

Two thirds, three halves; a half, a two:

each glass and its reflection, one.

The house counts five where you count four.

The knife comes home between the beats.

The rods are still.

The coil is quiet.

The can is full.

The chair is where you left it.

You never left it.

You stop singing.

Something keeps your time.
