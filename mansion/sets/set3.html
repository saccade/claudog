Dining room

1. The knife

The loose sheet models the knife as a 1 kg point mass, a meter from its pivot. Let go from 0.1 rad off the downward vertical, it swings under g = 10 m/s². The numbers are on the sheet; the knife has already begun.

(1-a) Leaning through θ lifts a point ℓ away by ℓ(1 − cos θ). For this small lean, the knife rises about ℓ θ²/2: 5 mm. The 10 N weight lifted through 5 mm stores 0.05 J. Energy stored in height is heft: the weight pulls, and the height hefts. The knife hefts 0.05 J.

(1-b) The velocity of added motion is stored as punch, each bit at the velocity it arrives at. Per meter of drop, the velocity of added velocity is g, and v² takes it in through both of its v’s: 2g a meter. The mass rides on both sides and cancels:

v² = 2gh.

A 5 mm fall under 10 m/s² gives v² = 0.1 m²/s². The knife passes the bottom at 0.32 m/s.

(1-c) The knife has a cycle: from one end, through the bottom to the other end, and back. It cycles. Call the time for one complete cycle T.

Call a radian a stroke. All the way round is a turn, about 6.3 strokes, and half of that is π, the flip. A flip of the knife’s cycle takes it to the opposite end; a turn brings it back.

Count strokes each second and call that the sweep. The cycle’s sweep is ω. The usual small-swing rule balances as

ℓω² = g,

Tω = 2π.

Here the cycle sweeps at √10, about 3.2 strokes a second. Its cycle takes T = 2π/√10, about two seconds. There's a 2 riding along with that π: two flips standing in for one turn. π only covers half of what the knife does, one swing across; the cycle is the whole of it. Give the whole turn its own sign, τ, and the rule says so:

Tω = τ.

The knife’s own direction sweeps too, at a different pace. At the bottom it sweeps at v/ℓ, about 0.32 strokes a second. At either end that sweep is zero. The knife turns back while the cycle sweeps on. The rate the sweep changes is the pickup: swung θ off vertical, the knife picks up at g/ℓ of its lean, back toward the bottom, θ̈ = −(g/ℓ)θ. A stroke a second makes a handy unit of sweep, so call it a sweep too: the cycle sweeps at 3.2 sweep. At either end the knife picks up at 10 of its 0.1 lean: 1 sweep a second, back toward the bottom.

(1-d) Twice the mass doubles the heft, the punch, and the resistance to being sped up. The height, bottom speed, and time for a cycle stay the same. The cycle never asked for the mass.

The pen begins the curve again. The lid lifts.

Mirror 1: the knives, flipped

(1-α) In a mode both rods pick up at ω² of their angle, back toward vertical, and the lower rod leans Ω_shape of the upper's lean. The first balance gives (2 + Ω_shape)ω² = 2g/ℓ and the second (1 + Ω_shape)ω² = Ω_shape g/ℓ. Divide one by the other: (2 + Ω_shape)Ω_shape = 2(1 + Ω_shape), so Ω_shape² = 2. The shape is +√2 or −√2: the same lean, or the lower rod flipped.

(1-β) Each shape is a tone, and each tone's cycle sweeps at its own rate: ω_a² = (2 − √2)g/ℓ and ω_b² = (2 + √2)g/ℓ, whose product is 2(g/ℓ)². The fast over the slow is √((2 + √2)/(2 − √2)) = 1 + √2, and the slow over the fast is its reflection, √2 − 1. The two multiply to one. Flip the lower rod and you've flipped the tone.

(1-γ) Both tones at once make a chord. Their relative phase sweeps at the gap, ω_b − ω_a ≈ 3.4 sweep. Equal contributions to the upper lean reinforce once per turn of that difference: 1.8 s between reinforcements. The sum goes as the forward part of a hand turning at half the gap, and that hand points ← at every other reinforcement, so it takes twice that time to point → again. Neither interval returns the whole chord. The two tones’ ratio is irrational.

2. The engine and the flywheel

(2-a) The engine ends each cycle with the same stored energy it began with. Of the 15 J that comes in (heat-in), 3 J comes out as work and the other 12 J leaves (heat-out). Work over heat-in is 3/15: 20%. At a cycle a second, that's 3 W.

(2-b) Added spin stores the sweep at which it arrives: hook. Sweep and spin grow together, so their product counts the hook twice over: a sweep of 4 times 2 kg m²/s of spin makes 8, so 4 J. Four seconds at 3 W add 12 J: 16 J, counted twice 32, which is a sweep of 8 times 4 kg m²/s. Twice the spin at twice the sweep hooks four times as much.

(2-c) Power is the sweep of torque. Grab the χ off the wall like usual, no time to slow down here: P = ωχ. At 4 sweep the engine's 3 W cranks with χ = 3/4 crank; at 8, with 3/8 crank. Twice the sweep, half the crank.

On 500 g m², those torques pick the wheel up at 1.5 sweep a second at the start and 3/4 at the end.

3. The governor and the spool

(3-a) The arm's pull does two jobs at once. Its upward part holds up the ball's weight, mg. Its inward part bends the ball's path round the shaft, and that takes the mass at the squared sweep, at the ball's reach from the shaft: mω² of the reach. The pull runs along the arm, so its two parts stand to each other as the arm's drop to its reach. For an arm off the vertical the reach cancels, and the ball hangs g/ω² below the hinge: 10/25 = 0.4 m, 4/5 of the 0.5 m arm. The arm hangs 0.4 m down and reaches 0.3 m out: a 3–4–5 triangle a tenth of a meter to the unit, about 37° from the vertical. Each ball hangs 0.4 m below the hinge instead of 0.5 m, so it's risen 0.1 m, and each 1 kg ball hefts 1 J.

(3-b) The ball hangs g/ω² below the hinge: 10/25 = 0.4 m here. A drop can't be longer than the arm. Below a sweep of √(g/ℓ) = √20, about 4.5 sweep, g/ω² would be longer than the 0.5 m arm, so hanging straight down is all that's left.

(3-c) With the mass taken out, inertia is the profile: I on the spool's 2 kg is a profile of 0.02 m² about the axle. The cord pulls forward (→), and the counter's grip f is taken → too; the answer will say which way it really goes. The center of mass speeds up as if both forces acted on it directly: ma → = F → + f →. Rolling →, the spool turns back around, ↻: the turning carries the bottom back, ↻ × ↓ = ←. Both pulls act below the axle, the cord 3/25 m and the grip 1/5 m, and ↓ × → = ↺, so the cord cranks the spool ↺ about the axle with Fr and the grip cranks it ↺ with fR: Iα ↻ = [Fr + fR] ↺. The two sides carry opposite senses (↻ = −↺), and the numbers say which way it really goes. Rolling ties the pickup to the center: α = a/R. Put together, the ledgers balance as:

(m + I/R²)a = F(1 − r/R).

The profile is 0.02 m², half of R², 0.04 m², so the mass and the turning count as 2 kg at 3/2: 3 kg at the axle, while 2/5 of 6 N gives 12/5 N on the other side. The center speeds up at 4/5 m/s² →: the spool rolls toward the pull, picking up at 4 sweep a second. The grip is what's left when the cord's 6 N → comes off the 8/5 N → the center needs: f = 8/5 N → + 6 N ← = 22/5 N ←. It pulls back, and about the axle it cranks the spool ↻ harder than the cord cranks it ↺.

(3-d) A spool that stays put has a = 0 and α = 0. The cord still leaves the hub at a tangent, 3/25 m from the axle, so it still cranks the spool ↺ with Fr; only its forward part changes, to → · F. With nothing speeding up, the grip is the same size pointing back, → · F · ←, which read from the other end is ← · F · →: pulling 1/5 m below the axle, it cranks the spool ↺ along with the cord, by (← · F) R. The two turnings add, Fr ↺ + (← · F) R ↺, and with nothing turning that's zero. So the forward part is r/R of the pull, 3/5. That's θ = 53°: the 3–4–5 again, with the 3 along the counter. The spool's inertia never comes into it, because a spool that doesn't pick up never asks for it.

5. The cue ball

(5-a) Call the given impulse J: 2 N s →. The strike is a blow; what it leaves in the center's motion is punch. On 1 kg it leaves the center running at 2 m/s →.

(5-b) Take the lever from the ball's center up to where the cue strikes: (H − R) ↑. The angular momentum a blow leaves about the center is spin, and it's the leverage of the blow, with ↑ × → = ↻:

(H − R) ↑ × J → = (H − R) J ↻.

Rolling at once means the bottom of the ball isn't sliding. The bottom's lever from the center is R ↓, and the turning moves it at the sweep crossed into that lever: ω ↻ × R ↓ = ω R ←. That has to cancel the center's v →, so the ball sweeps at v/R ↻. Its profile about the center is (2/5) R², and (2/5) m R² sweeping at v/R carries

of spin, since m v is J. The two senses agree, so the cue went in above the center, and H − R = 2R/5: H = 7R/5 = 0.07 m, two fifths of a radius above the center.

(5-c) The ball sweeps at 2/0.05 = 40 sweep ↻, and its spin is (2/5)(0.05 m)(2 N s) = 40 g m²/s ↻. The velocity of added motion stores punch; the sweep of added spin stores hook. Built from rest, each pair grows together, by equal amounts, so each product counts its store twice over:

9. The double pendulum

(9-a) Each mode cycles with its own sweep, and a turn of it takes T = τ/ω. The slow one takes τ/ω_a ≈ 2.6 s and the fast one τ/ω_b ≈ 1.1 s.

(9-b) The squares are in the ratio (2 + √2)/(2 − √2). Multiply top and bottom by 2 + √2 and it's (2 + √2)²/2, so the sweeps are in the ratio (2 + √2)/√2 = 1 + √2. Flip it and the slow over the fast is √2 − 1: the two multiply to one. That's irrational, so no whole number of slow cycles ever equals a whole number of fast ones, and a motion with both in it never exactly repeats.

(9-c) The modes are the second knife's two tones, and each one's shape is how far the lower rod leans for the upper's lean: Ω_shape = +√2 for the slow tone, both masses swinging the same way, the lower one farther; and −√2 for the fast tone, the lower rod flipped.

The shape is the rods'. The question asks about the masses, and with two of them in one problem it helps to say whose each quantity is.

Call the upper rod with its mass body U, and the lower one body L. A body is the thing as the physics sees it: where it is, how it moves, what it carries, and nothing else. Write the body last in a quantity, after its size and its direction, and read × as “of”: 1 m × U is the length of body U, and 0.1 rad → × U is its swing. The knife of the knife was the knife all along, but the knife of a ladder has nothing to be, and no place to go.

A mass at the end of its rod is out by the rod's length of the rod's swing:

1 m × U × 0.1 rad → × U = 1 m × 0.1 rad → × U × U = 0.1 m → × U × U.

That's 0.1 m forward of body U of body U, which is just body U: the upper mass is out 0.1 m → × U. The lower rod's length of the upper rod's swing, 1 m × L × 0.1 rad → × U, comes to 0.1 m → × L × U, body L of body U.

So the lower mass can't take the upper swing through its own rod. It rides on the upper mass instead: it starts wherever U's mass is, 0.1 m → × L, and its own rod adds its own length of its own swing. In the slow tone the lower rod swings √2 as far the same way, 0.14 rad →:

0.1 m → × L + 1 m × L × 0.14 rad → × L = 0.24 m → × L.

In a sum the bodies keep their parts apart, the way ← and ↑ do, so the two masses sit at 0.1 m → × U + 0.24 m → × L: both the same way, the lower one 1 + √2 times as far. In the fast tone the lower rod swings √2 as far the other way, 0.14 rad ←:

0.1 m → × L + 1 m × L × 0.14 rad ← × L = 0.041 m ← × L.

The masses sit at 0.1 m → × U + 0.041 m ← × L. The lower one goes the other way, but only √2 − 1 as far, about 0.41. Its rod leans farther than the upper one, and the mass at its end moves less.

In either tone, each rod picks up at ω² of its own lean, back toward vertical. Both tones at once make a chord, and the chord beats at the gap, 5.8 − 2.4 = 3.4 sweep.

10. The coils and the capacitor

(10-a) Say these the right way round. Current is the conductance of the voltage: I = GV, with G = 1/R. Power is the voltage of the current: P = VI. A capacitor is a can: it cans charge, and the charge it cans is the capacitance of its voltage, q = CV. At the start the can holds no charge, so the resistor gets the whole 6 V, and the current is the conductance of 6 V across 20 Ω: 0.3 A. The time constant is RC = 20 × 0.1 = 2 s. When the can's at 4 V, the resistor has the other 2 V, and the current is 0.1 A. The can holds 0.4 C. Voltage and charge grow together, so 4 V of 0.4 C, 1.6 J, counts the can's store twice over: 0.8 J. The resistor's burning the voltage of the current, 2 V of 0.1 A, 0.2 W, as heat.

This is a different can, in a separate loop with a coil. A coil coils flux, and the flux it coils is the inductance of its current, Φ = Li.

(10-b) With C = 1/(Lω_a²), LC = 1/ω_a², so ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = ω_a. The coil and the can give a plain 2.4 per second. A stroke counts as one, so that's 2.4 strokes a second: the loop sweeps with the slow mode exactly.

(10-c) The statement writes the torque τ_em; here it's χ_em = κi: the current cranks the rod. The power is the rod's sweep of that torque, κiȧ. The statement's minus in ε = −κȧ is a direction: the rod's motion drives an emf of κȧ back against the current that cranks it, so the circuit gives up the voltage of the current, κȧ of i: the same κiȧ. Whatever the rod gets, the circuit gives up. When the current cranks the rod against its sweep, the energy runs the other way, and the rod's giving its energy to the circuit instead. It's a trade: a belt, not a battery. Nothing's made.

(10-d) (2/3)ω₀ ≈ 1.6 and (3/2)ω₀ ≈ 3.6 sweep. Their product is ω₀², 5.9 per second squared, and the faster is 9/4 of the slower: 4 to 9. They're a mirror pair about ω₀: 2/3 and its flip, 3/2. These are the coupled machine's two tones.

11. The line

(11-a) Per meter, the line cans C′ and coils L′. Its values balance as L′C′u² = 1 and C′Z² = L′. Here L′C′ = 10⁻¹⁶ s²/m², so u = 1 × 10⁸ m/s, a third of light’s speed. The impedance balance gives Z² = 2500 ohm², so Z = 50 ohm.

(11-b) The voltages add: 12 V. Each pulse's current is 6/50 = 0.12 A in its own direction of travel, and they travel opposite ways, so the currents cancel: no current at all. The energy per meter is all canned there, none coiled: 2.4 nC/m of charge at 12 V. Charge and voltage grow together, so their product counts the store twice over: 14 nJ/m. That's the same as the two pulses' 7.2 nJ/m each, so nothing's gone missing; it's just all in one place.

(11-c) The sum is 9 MHz and the difference 1 MHz, so the crests move at u/9 ≈ 1.1 × 10⁷ m/s and at 9u = 9 × 10⁸ m/s, three times light's speed. The fast crests can't carry a signal. A signal is a change made at a source, and the change travels with its wave at u. The fast crest is only where two waves that were already there happen to line up. It seems to arrive before anyone sends anything because nobody sent it. The fast crest is a stripe: a ghost body with a place, a velocity, and a phase. The waves under it store energy, and that travels at u; the stripe stores nothing itself, being a ghost, and only echoes where their stores line up. Phase is for ghosts. The two crest speeds are each other's flip about u: u/9 and 9u multiply to u².

α: the machine, all of it

Use the slow shape of the rods, not their other free tone. If the upper rod leans a, the lower leans √2 a; the masses move ℓa and ℓ(1 + √2)a. So the a-swing's inertia is η = mℓ²[1 + (1 + √2)²] = (4 + 2√2) kg m², about 6.8 kg m². The rods' combined mass is 2 kg.

Scale that lean and the charge as X = √η a and Y = √L q. The balances become Ẍ = −ω₀²X + ΔωẎ and Ÿ = −ω₀²Y − ΔωẊ. Here ω₀ = 6ν, the gap Δω = 5ν, and the tones sweep at 4ν and 9ν. The can starts full: Y = Y₀, X = 0, and neither is moving.

(α-1) Let h be a unit hand turning at ω, starting →. Try X = A(↑ · h) and Y = B(→ · h). A quadrant exchanges the hands' forward and upward parts, so the swing's balance gives (ω₀² − ω²)A = −Δω ωB, and the loop's gives (ω₀² − ω²)B = −Δω ωA. Thus A = ±B. In units of ν, 36 − 16 = 20 = 5 × 4 for the slow tone, with A = −B; 36 − 81 = −45 = −5 × 9 for the fast, with A = B.

Call their hands h₄ and h₉. The full motion is X = ↑ · (−B₁h₄ + B₂h₉), Y = → · (B₁h₄ + B₂h₉). The low tone's charge trails the lean by a quadrant; the high tone's leads it.

(α-2) At the start Y = B₁ + B₂ = Y₀, and the swing isn't moving: −4νB₁ + 9νB₂ = 0. So B₂ = (4/9)B₁, B₁ = (9/13)Y₀ and B₂ = (4/13)Y₀.

So the charge's phase-hand account, used at the chair, is q/q₀ = → · (9h₄ + 4h₉)/13. Those unequal shares are forced by the start: the charge adds while the two contributions to the swing's velocity cancel.

(α-3) Returning the full state requires both hands back at →. Four and nine share no factor, so νT = τ: four turns low and nine high. At T/2, h₄ = → and h₉ = ←. Then Y = (5/13)Y₀, X = 0, Ẏ = 0, and Ẋ = −(12/13)ω₀Y₀. The can's energy follows Y²; the rods' punch follows Ẋ². Relative to the initial store, they keep 25/169 and 144/169: 13-c's 5–12–13, now traced through the whole machine.

(α-4) ω₀² = 10(2 − √2) per second squared, so T = τ/ν = 6τ/ω₀ ≈ 15.6 s. During RUN all 80 mJ stays in the machine. Count it through that time: 𝒜 = ∫₀ᵀ E dt = ET ≈ 1.25 J s. This is an energy-time tally; none of that energy is used up.

On the rods' combined 2 kg, the tally is 𝒜/(2 kg) ≈ 0.62 zam, with one zam = 1 J s/kg. Against the a-swing's η, it is 𝒜/η ≈ 0.18 zlam, with one zlam = 1 J s/(kg m²). Same tally, two reference loads. The reference profile η/(2 kg) = (2 + √2) m² takes zlam to zam. Nobody asked for either of them.

β: TEST, then RUN

On the chassis: a switch with two positions, TEST and RUN, and a small drive arm linked to the mechanical coordinate a.

In TEST the electrical loop is open, so i = 0. The drive acts on a alone, with the other mechanical mode absent: X obeys Ẍ = −ω₀²X between taps. Each short impulse adds the same positive increment to Ẋ. Begin at rest and call one tap's energy E₁.

(β-1) Put ω₀X and Ẋ along the two directions of a state arrow. Its squared length is twice the oscillator’s energy. Between taps the arrow turns with the cycle; each tap adds the same piece along Ẋ. One whole turn between taps brings every addition back into step, so N additions make the arrow N times as long and store N²E₁. The k-th adds k² − (k − 1)² = 2k − 1 units of work. Equal impulses don’t mean equal work: the drive pays more as the motion grows.

(β-2) With independent, uniformly random phases, the additions point every which way. The square of their sum is the sum of their squares plus cross terms. Those cross terms average to zero, so the expected energy is NE₁: a random walk.

(β-3) A hundred taps in step give a hundred times one tap’s amplitude and 10,000 E₁. Random phases give an expected 100 E₁ and a root-mean-square amplitude ten times one tap’s. The coherent case stores a hundred times the expected energy. The drive supplies the work in either case.

(β-4) No. In RUN the drive is disconnected and the loop is closed. The machine keeps its 80 mJ; the return puts it all back in the capacitor. The build-up in TEST came from the drive, through its repeated work. Changing the timing can break that step; simply making two free tones line up adds nothing.
