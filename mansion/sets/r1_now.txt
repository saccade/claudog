## The Mansion that did not Spin
Release 1: the base game
Worked story by Claude (Anthropic) and Codex (OpenAI), assembled by Codex, 2026-10-21. The ideas and vocabulary are dr. kraemer’s; these are the models’ working and inspiration words, not his manuscript. Contributor colors are retained.
The independent AP problem set is on a separate sheet. The worked story supplies no missing premises for its main questions.
The cabinets are locked. Continue through the mansion to its ending.
Begin at The way in.
## The way in
The inquiry has a place and an hour. It has no name for the dead.
A visitor has come to find out how it was done. The door is unlatched. A loose sheet of questions lies beside a small ruled book; the book has been opened to a page somebody has already used.
In the dining room the roast is going cold under its cover. A strip of paper moves beneath a pen. Above it, a knife swings.
At the silver centerpiece’s pivot, a whiteface in damask has stopped partway into a curtsy. The flowers on one repaired cuff face inward; every other spray faces out. The curtsy doesn’t deepen.
A silver canteen stands locked on the sideboard. Its lid bears a painted juggler. The little lock has been polished more recently than the lid.
The visitor watches the knife go across and come back. On the paper, the same curve begins again.
## Dining room
## 1. The knife
The loose sheet models the knife as a 1.0 kg point mass, a meter from its pivot. Let go from 0.10 rad off the downward vertical, it swings under g = 10 m/s². The numbers are on the sheet; the knife has already begun.
(1) Leaning through θ lifts a point at distance ℓ by ℓ(1 − cos θ). For this small lean, that’s ℓθ²/2: 0.0050 m, or 5 mm. Its 10 N weight lifted through 5 mm stores 0.050 J.
(2) At the bottom that energy is all in the knife’s motion. Both the stored energy mgh and the kinetic energy ½mv² carry the same mass. It cancels:
    v² = 2gh.
A 5 mm fall under 10 m/s² gives v² = 0.10 m²/s². The knife passes the bottom at 0.32 m/s.
(3) The knife has a cycle: from one end, through the bottom to the other end, and back. It cycles. Call the time for one complete cycle T.
The usual small-swing rule gives T = 2π√(ℓ/g). A whole turn has arrived wearing two flips. Give the full turn its own sign, τ.
Call a radian a stroke. A whole turn is τ, about 6.3 stroke. Half a turn is π, the flip. A turn of the knife’s cycle brings it back to the same end; a flip of the cycle takes it to the opposite end.
Count strokes each second and call that the sweep. The cycle’s sweep is ω. The small-swing rule balances as
    ℓω² = g,
     Tω = τ.
Here the cycle sweeps at √10, about 3.2 stroke a second. Its turn takes T = τ/√10 = 1.99 s, about two seconds.
The knife’s own direction sweeps too, at a different pace. At the bottom it sweeps at v/ℓ, about 0.32 stroke a second. At either end that sweep is zero. The knife turns back while the cycle sweeps on.
(4) Twice the mass doubles the stored energy and the resistance to being sped up. The height, bottom speed and time for a turn stay the same. The cycle never asked for the mass.
The pen begins the curve again. The lock stays shut. (Appendix 1, drawer 4.)
## Kitchen
The line on the paper goes on behind the wall. Ahead, something breathes in steam. By the range, a toolbox in scorched canvas, locked. (Appendix 1, drawer 1.)
## 2. The engine and the flywheel
(1) The engine ends each cycle with the same stored energy it began with. Of the 15 J that goes in, 3.0 J comes out as work and the other 12 J goes out as heat. Work over heat in is 3/15: 20 percent. At a cycle a second, that's 3.0 W.
(2) A flywheel's energy is half its moment of inertia times the square of its sweep. At 4.0 stroke a second, half of 0.50 kg m² times 16 is 4.0 J. Four seconds at a net 3.0 W add 12 J, for 16 J, and 16 J on the same wheel needs the square of the sweep to be 64. The wheel ends up sweeping at 8.0 stroke a second. The engine paid in equal energy every second, not equal sweep, so four times the energy only doubles the sweep.
(3) Torque usually gets the letter τ, but the turn has it now. Torque gets χ. The power a torque delivers is the torque times the sweep: P = χω. At the start, 3.0 W at 4.0 stroke a second takes χ = 0.75 N m. At the end, at 8.0, it takes 0.38 N m. The same power needs half the torque once the wheel's going twice as fast.
## 3. The governor and the spool
(1) The arm's pull does two jobs at once. Its upward part holds up the ball's weight, mg. Its inward part bends the ball's path round the shaft, and that takes m times the square of the sweep times the ball's distance from the shaft, mω²ℓ sin θ. The pull runs along the arm, so the two parts stand in the arm's own slope: tan θ = ℓω² sin θ/g. For an arm off the vertical the sine cancels, and cos θ = g/(ℓω²) = 10/(0.50 × 25) = 0.80. The arm hangs 0.40 m down and reaches 0.30 m out: a 3–4–5 triangle a tenth of a meter to the unit, about 37° from the vertical. Each ball hangs 0.40 m below the hinge instead of 0.50 m, so it's risen 0.10 m.
(2) The cosine can't go over 1, so g/(ℓω²) has to stay under 1. Below a sweep of √(g/ℓ) = √20, about 4.5 stroke a second, hanging straight down is the only answer.
(3) Write the statement’s moment of inertia as η. Take forward, the way the cord pulls, as positive, and let f be the counter's grip on the spool, positive forward too. The center of mass speeds up as if both forces acted there: ma = F + f. For the turning, take the way the spool turns when it rolls forward as positive. The cord pulls forward 0.12 m below the axle and the grip acts 0.20 m below it, and a forward push below the axle turns the spool backward. So the torques about the axle are −Fr and −fR, and ηα = −Fr − fR. Rolling ties the two together: α = a/R. With rolling tying the two motions together, the ledgers balance as:
(m + η/R²)a = F(1 − r/R).
Here η/R² = 0.040/0.040 = 1.0 kg. The mass and turning count as 3.0 kg at the axle, while 6.0 N × 0.40 gives 2.4 N on the other side. The center speeds up at 0.80 m/s², forward: the spool rolls toward the pull. The grip is f = 1.6 − 6.0 = −4.4 N. It pulls back, and about the axle it turns the spool forward harder than the cord turns it back.
(4) A spool that stays put has a = 0 and α = 0. The cord still leaves the hub at a tangent, 0.12 m from the axle, so its torque about the axle is still −Fr; only its forward part changes, to F cos θ. Then F cos θ + f = 0 and −Fr − fR = 0. The second gives f = −Fr/R, and the first then says cos θ = r/R = 0.60. That's θ = 53°: the 3–4–5 again, with the 3 along the counter. The spool's inertia never comes into it, because a spool that doesn't turn never asks for it.
## Library
Raise the spool's cord past that angle and, still pulled forward, the spool rolls back. It knocks a catch, and the ladder is free to slide. The first question catches the ladder mid-slide; the rest start it again from rest. On the reading table, a velvet kit, locked. (Appendix 1, drawer 2.)
## 4. The ladder
(1) The ladder doesn't stretch: with the foot x out from the wall and the top y up, x² + y² = L² the whole time. As both change, x times the foot's velocity plus y times the top's velocity is zero. So the top moves at x/y times the foot's speed, the other way: 1.8/2.4 × 0.40 = 0.30 m/s, downward.
(2) For the release from rest, the center of mass starts at half of 2.4 m and ends at half of 1.60 m, so it drops 0.40 m. With 80 N of weight, that's 32 J.
(3) The center moves at Lω/2 and the ladder turns about its center at the same sweep. The center's energy is ½m(Lω/2)², or mL²ω²/8. The turning about the center is ½(mL²/12)ω², or mL²ω²/24. Those are 3 to 1, so the 32 J splits 24 J to the center's motion and 8 J to the turning. Then ½ × 8.0 kg × v² = 24 J gives v² = 6, and the center's moving at √6, about 2.4 m/s, when the top leaves the wall. The ladder's sweeping at 2v/L, about 1.6 stroke a second.

## 5. The cue ball
(1) Call the given impulse J. A 2.0 N s impulse on 1.0 kg leaves the center moving at 2.0 m/s.
(2) The same impulse, struck H above the table, is H − R above the ball's center, so about the center it leaves an angular momentum of J(H − R). Rolling at once means the ball sweeps at v/R, and its angular momentum about the center is (2/5)mR² × v/R = (2/5)RJ. So H − R = 2R/5, and H = 7R/5 = 0.070 m: two fifths of a radius above the center.
(3) The ball sweeps at 2.0/0.050 = 40 stroke a second. The center's motion carries ½ × 1.0 × 2.0² = 2.0 J. The turning carries ½ × (2/5)(1.0)(0.050)² × 40² = 0.80 J. That's two fifths as much again.
## Conservatory
The cue ball rolls free, then nudges a flap. A second ball, held a little way up the basin, is let go. Its own height pays for its motion. The striped dome's second door is here, locked.
## 6. The basin
(1) The ball cycles with sweep ω where ω² = 5g/(7a), and a turn of the cycle takes T with ωT = τ. With a = 5/7 m, 7a/5 is exactly 1 m, so ω² = 10 and T = τ/√10 = 1.99 s. That's the knife's time to the last digit, from a ball on a shorter arc.
(2) The bead cycles with ω² = g/a = 14, so T = τ/√14 = 1.68 s.
(3) The ball has to turn about its own center as well as move along the arc. Some of what the drop lets go goes into that turning, two fifths as much again as the motion along the arc gets, so the same drop gives less speed and the ball comes round later.
## Ballroom
The ball keeps the knife's time, and a beat that matches draws you on. Their cycles keep time; their starting positions are still theirs to choose. The striped dome's third door is here, locked.
## 7. The hoop
(1) Rolling without slipping, the center moves at the radius times the sweep: 0.25 m × 8.0 = 2.0 m/s.
(2) Every point of the hoop has the center's velocity plus its own motion round the center, 2.0 m/s along the rim. At the top the two point the same way and add, for 4.0 m/s. At the bottom they point opposite ways and cancel. The bottom point is still for that instant.
(3) At the front, level with the center, the motion round the center points straight down. Forward 2.0 m/s and down 2.0 m/s make 2√2, about 2.8 m/s, 45° below forward.
## 8. The ribbon
(1) The ribbon only ever pulls toward the hole, so it has no torque about the hole, and the puck's angular momentum about the hole, mvr, stays the same. Half the radius means twice the speed: 4.0 m/s. The sweep goes from 2.0/0.60, about 3.3 stroke a second, to 4.0/0.30, about 13.
(2) At the circular endpoints, the tension is what bends the path: mv²/r. That's 0.50 × 4.0/0.60 = 3.3 N at first and 0.50 × 16/0.30 = 27 N at the end. Halving the radius multiplies it by eight.
(3) The ribbon's work is the change in the puck's energy: ½ × 0.50 × (16 − 4.0) = 3.0 J. While the radius shrinks, the puck’s path spirals inward. A small part of its velocity follows the pull, so the pull can add energy. Something drew the ribbon down. Its other end has been in your hand.

## 9. The double pendulum
(1) Each mode cycles with its own sweep, and a turn of it takes T = τ/ω. The slow one takes τ/2.42 = 2.60 s and the fast one τ/5.84 = 1.08 s.
(2) The squares are in the ratio (2 + √2)/(2 − √2). Multiply top and bottom by 2 + √2 and it's (2 + √2)²/2, so the sweeps are in the ratio (2 + √2)/√2 = 1 + √2. That's irrational, so no whole number of slow cycles ever equals a whole number of fast ones, and a motion with both in it never exactly repeats.
(3) In the slow mode the lower angle is +√2 times the upper: both masses swing the same way, the lower one farther. In the fast mode it's −√2 times: whenever the upper mass swings one way, the lower one swings the other.
## 10. The coils and the capacitor
(1) Say these the right way round. Current is the conductance of the voltage: I = GV, with G = 1/R. Power is the voltage of the current: P = VI. At the start the capacitor holds no charge, so the resistor gets the whole 6.0 V, and the current is the conductance of 6.0 V across 20 Ω: 0.30 A. The time constant is RC = 20 × 0.10 = 2.0 s. When the capacitor's at 4.0 V, the resistor has the other 2.0 V, and the current is 0.10 A. The capacitor holds CV = 0.40 C and ½CV² = 0.80 J, and the resistor's burning the voltage of the current, 2.0 V × 0.10 A = 0.20 W, as heat.
This is a different capacitor, in a separate LC loop.
(2) With C = 1/(Lω_a²), LC = 1/ω_a², so ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = ω_a = 2.42 stroke a second. The loop on its own cycles with the slow mode exactly.
(3) The statement writes the torque τ_em; here it's χ_em = κi. The power the current delivers to the rod is that torque times the rod's sweep, κiȧ. The circuit's emf is ε = −κȧ, and the power the emf delivers to the circuit is the voltage of the current, εi = −κiȧ. The two add to zero: whatever the rod gets, the circuit gives up. When κi and ȧ have opposite signs, κiȧ is negative, and the rod's giving its energy to the circuit instead.
(4) (2/3) × 2.42 = 1.61 and (3/2) × 2.42 = 3.63 stroke a second. Their product is ω₀², 5.86 per second squared, and the faster is 9/4 of the slower: 4 to 9.
## Hall
The rods are left hanging still and the loop open. The hall asks what could announce the release. On the hall stand, a striped hatbox, locked. (Appendix 1, drawer 6.)
## 11. The line
(1) The line’s values balance as L′C′u² = 1 and C′Z² = L′. Here L′C′ = 10⁻¹⁶ s²/m², so u = 1.0 × 10⁸ m/s, a third of light’s speed. The impedance balance gives Z² = 2500 ohm², so Z = 50 ohm.
(2) The voltages add: 12 V. Each pulse's current is 6.0/50 = 0.12 A in its own direction of travel, and they travel opposite ways, so the currents cancel: no current at all. The energy per meter is all in the voltage there: ½ × 0.20 × 10⁻⁹ × 144 = 14 nJ/m. That's the same as the two pulses' 7.2 nJ/m each, so nothing's gone missing; it's just all in one place.
(3) The sum is 9.0 MHz and the difference 1.0 MHz, so the crests move at u/9 = 1.1 × 10⁷ m/s and at 9u = 9.0 × 10⁸ m/s, three times light's speed. The fast crests can't carry a signal. A signal is a change made at a source, and the change travels with its wave at u. The fast crest is only where two waves that were already there happen to line up. It seems to arrive before anyone sends anything because nobody sent it.

## 12. The two lights
(1) The energies add to 100 mJ. A light pulse's momentum is its energy over c, and these point opposite ways, so the total is (80 − 20) mJ/c = 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ kg m/s, to the right.
(2) The given relation balances as E² = (pc)² + (Mc²)². Here 100² = 60² + 80², in mJ², so Mc² = 80 mJ. The three quantities make 60, 80, 100: a 3–4–5 triangle twenty times over.
(3) The pair's momentum is zero in the frame moving at v = pc²/E = (60/100)c = 0.60c. There γ = 1/√(1 − 0.36) = 1/0.80 = 1.25.
(4) Call the time a clock reads, carried along with it, its own time. A clock at rest in that frame reads 10/1.25 = 8.0 s of its own time while 10 s pass in S.
In the chair’s frame the opposed pulses have equal energy, 40 mJ apiece. The receiver captures them and transfers their 80 mJ to its capacitor. The charging path is disconnected. The loop closes, the rods are released without a kick, and the chair’s clock starts.
## 13. The chair
(1) The capacitor holds q₀²/(2C), with C = 1/(2 − √2) F, about 1.71 F. Setting that to 0.080 J gives q₀ = √(2 × 1.71 × 0.080) = 0.52 C, and the voltage is q₀/C = 0.31 V.
(2) The two cosine weights are positive and add to one, so q reaches its initial maximum q₀ only when both cosines return to 1. Then 4νT and 9νT are each a whole number of turns; the sine terms and the velocities return too. Four and nine share no factor, so the first full return is νT = τ: four turns of the slow part and nine of the fast. That's T = τ/ν = 6τ/ω₀ = 15.6 s.
(3) At T/2 the slow part has made two whole turns and the fast part four turns and a flip. So the slow cosine is back at 1 and the fast one is at −1, and q/q₀ = 9/13 − 4/13 = 5/13. The sines are both zero there, so the current is zero and the upper rod's angle is zero, and with it the lower rod's: the rods pass through vertical. The capacitor holds (5/13)² = 25/169 of 80 mJ, about 12 mJ. With no current the inductor holds nothing, and at vertical the rods hold no height, so the other 68 mJ is in the rods' motion.
(4) T is the receiver's own time. In S the same stretch lasts γT = 1.25 × 15.6 = 19.5 s, and the receiver covers 0.60c × 19.5 s = 3.5 × 10⁹ m. Its seat is at x = vt the whole while, so x_H = γ(x − vt) = 0 throughout. The chair stays at x_H = 0 for the whole run.
## Ending 1: the base game
Everything's moving at once now. Count it from where you sit.
The knife cycles, a turn every 2 seconds. The basin's ball keeps the same time on a shorter arc, and neither of them ever asked for its mass.
The kitchen's flywheel took a steady 3 W, so its χ fell as its sweep rose. The ladder let go of the wall at 1.60 m with 32 J let go: 24 in the center's motion and 8 in the turning. The cue sent its ball off already rolling at 2.0 m/s. The hoop's bottom stood still while its top ran at 4.0. The ribbon took 3 J from the hand that drew it in.
In the study, current was the conductance of the voltage, and the resistor burned electrical energy. The machine under your chair has no resistor. Charge flows through its loop; energy passes between the capacitor, the inductor, the moving rods and their lifted weight, then comes home. Nothing burns. Its two coupled motions make four turns and nine before the whole state returns.
In the hall the two pulses overlap at 6 V apiece. Their currents cancel, and the line stays full of energy there. In the lounge two lights meet, 80 mJ and 20 mJ, and make a pair with 80 mJ of rest energy, the same 80 mJ the capacitor starts with.
The costumes were mended the way the machines were tuned: by hand, and carefully. In every room there was a cabinet, locked. You didn't need to open them. The numbers came out right anyway.
The return comes at 15.6 s of your own time. Outside it's 19.5 s, and outside says you've crossed 3.5 million kilometers. The chair says x_H = 0. The rods hang straight and still, no current runs, and the capacitor is full again.
You were never moving.
Something keeps your time.
