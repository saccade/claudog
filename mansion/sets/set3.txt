## Dining room
## 1. The knife
The loose sheet models the knife as a 1 kg point mass, a meter from its pivot. Let go from 1/10 rad off the downward vertical, it swings under g = 10 m/s². The numbers are on the sheet; the knife has already begun.
(1-a) Leaning through θ lifts a point ℓ away by ℓ(1 − cos θ). For this small lean, that’s ℓ θ²/2: 0.005 m, or 5 mm. The 10 N weight lifted through 5 mm stores 1/20 J. Energy stored in height is heft: the weight pulls, and the height hefts. The knife hefts 1/20 J.
(1-b) At the bottom the heft has all gone into the knife’s motion. Energy in a body's motion is its punch, ½mv². The heft mgh and the punch ½mv² carry the same mass. It cancels:
v² = 2gh.
A 5 mm fall under 10 m/s² gives v² = 1/10 m²/s². The knife passes the bottom at 0.32 m/s.
(1-c) The knife has a cycle: from one end, through the bottom to the other end, and back. It cycles. Call the time for one complete cycle T.
Call a radian a stroke. All the way round is a turn, about 6.3 strokes, and half of that is π, the flip. A flip of the knife’s cycle takes it to the opposite end; a turn brings it back.
Count strokes each second and call that the sweep. The cycle’s sweep is ω. The usual small-swing rule balances as
ℓω² = g,
Tω = 2π.
Here the cycle sweeps at √10, about 3.2 strokes a second. Its cycle takes T = 2π/√10, about two seconds. There's a 2 riding along with that π: two flips standing in for one turn. π only covers half of what the knife does, one swing across; the cycle is the whole of it. Give the whole turn its own sign, τ, and the rule says so:
Tω = τ.
The knife’s own direction sweeps too, at a different pace. At the bottom it sweeps at v/ℓ, about 0.32 strokes a second. At either end that sweep is zero. The knife turns back while the cycle sweeps on. The rate the sweep changes is the pickup: swung θ off vertical, the knife picks up at g/ℓ of its lean, back toward the bottom, θ̈ = −(g/ℓ)θ. A stroke a second makes a handy unit of sweep, so call it a sweep too: the cycle sweeps at 3.2 sweep. At either end the knife picks up at 10 of its 1/10 lean: 1 sweep a second, back toward the bottom.
(1-d) Twice the mass doubles the heft, the punch, and the resistance to being sped up. The height, bottom speed, and time for a cycle stay the same. The cycle never asked for the mass.
The pen begins the curve again. The lid lifts.

## Drawer d: the second knife's two tones

(d-1) In a mode both rods pick up at ω² of their angle, back toward vertical. The first balance gives (2 ± √2)ω²θ₁ = 2(g/ℓ)θ₁, so ω² = 2g/((2 ± √2)ℓ) = (2 ∓ √2)g/ℓ. The second balance gives the same two. The shape where the lower rod leans the same way, +√2, is the slow one.
(d-2) ω² = 10(2 − √2) = 5.9 and 10(2 + √2) = 34 per second squared, so the slow mode cycles at 2.4 sweep and the fast one at 5.8. Their ratio is (2 + √2)/√2 = 1 + √2. That's irrational, so a motion with both modes never quite repeats.

## Mirror 1: the knives, flipped
A long mirror in which the second knife hangs upside down.
(1-α) In a mode both rods pick up at ω² of their angle, back toward vertical, and the lower rod leans Ω_shape of the upper's lean. The first balance gives (2 + Ω_shape)ω² = 2g/ℓ and the second (1 + Ω_shape)ω² = Ω_shape g/ℓ. Divide one by the other: (2 + Ω_shape)Ω_shape = 2(1 + Ω_shape), so Ω_shape² = 2. The shape is +√2 or −√2: the same lean, or the lower rod flipped.
(1-β) Each shape is a tone, and each tone's cycle sweeps at its own rate: ω_a² = (2 − √2)g/ℓ and ω_b² = (2 + √2)g/ℓ, whose product is 2(g/ℓ)². The fast over the slow is √((2 + √2)/(2 − √2)) = 1 + √2, and the slow over the fast is its reflection, √2 − 1. The two multiply to one. Flip the lower rod and you've flipped the tone.
(1-γ) Both tones at once make a chord. Their relative phase sweeps at the gap, ω_b − ω_a = 3.4 sweep. Equal contributions to the upper lean reinforce once per turn of that difference: 1.8 s between reinforcements. The sum goes as the forward part of a hand turning at half the gap, and that hand points ← at every other reinforcement, so it takes twice that time to point → again. Neither interval returns the whole chord. The two tones’ ratio is irrational.
Trapdoor, the swing side: in the floor under the mirror, and through it the same two rods, full size, on a black iron chassis with a chipped ceramic insulator. They're humming. It's nailed down.

## 2. The engine and the flywheel
(2-a) The engine ends each cycle with the same stored energy it began with. Of the 15 J that comes in (heat-in), 3 J comes out as work and the other 12 J leaves (heat-out). Work over heat-in is 3/15: 20%. At a cycle a second, that's 3 W.
(2-b) Energy in turning is hook: a flywheel hooks half its moment of inertia at the squared sweep. 1/2 kg m² sweeping at 4 sweep hooks 4 J. Four seconds at a net 3 W add 12 J of hook, for 16 J, and 16 J of hook on the same wheel needs the squared sweep to be 64. The wheel ends up sweeping at 8. The engine paid in equal hook every second, not equal sweep, so four times the hook only doubles the sweep.
(2-c) Power is the sweep of torque. Grab the χ off the wall like usual, no time to slow down here: P = ωχ. At 4 sweep the engine's 3 W cranks with χ = 3/4 crank; at 8, with 3/8 crank. Twice the sweep, half the crank.
When the wheel goes twice as fast, the same power cranks just half as hard. On 1/2 kg m², those torques pick the wheel up at 3/2 sweep a second at the start and 3/4 at the end.
## 3. The governor and the spool
(3-a) The arm's pull does two jobs at once. Its upward part holds up the ball's weight, mg. Its inward part bends the ball's path round the shaft, and that takes the mass at the squared sweep, at the ball's reach from the shaft: mω² of the reach. The pull runs along the arm, so its two parts stand to each other as the arm's drop to its reach. For an arm off the vertical the reach cancels, and the ball hangs g/ω² below the hinge: 10/25 = 2/5 m, 4/5 of the 1/2 m arm. The arm hangs 2/5 m down and reaches 3/10 m out: a 3–4–5 triangle a tenth of a meter to the unit, about 37° from the vertical. Each ball hangs 2/5 m below the hinge instead of 1/2 m, so it's risen 1/10 m, and each 1 kg ball hefts 1 J.
(3-b) The ball hangs g/ω² below the hinge: 10/25 = 2/5 m here. A drop can't be longer than the arm. Below a sweep of √(g/ℓ) = √20, about 4.5 sweep, g/ω² would be longer than the 1/2 m arm, so hanging straight down is all that's left.
(3-c) With the mass taken out, inertia is the profile: I on the spool's 2 kg is a profile of 1/50 m² about the axle. The cord pulls forward (→), and the counter's grip f is taken → too; the answer will say which way it really goes. The center of mass speeds up as if both forces acted on it directly: ma → = F → + f →. Rolling →, the spool turns back around, ↻: the turning carries the bottom back, ↻ × ↓ = ←. Both pulls act below the axle, the cord 0.12 m and the grip 1/5 m, and ↓ × → = ↺, so the cord cranks the spool ↺ about the axle with Fr and the grip cranks it ↺ with fR: Iα ↻ = [Fr + fR] ↺. The two sides carry opposite senses (↻ = −↺), and the numbers say which way it really goes. Rolling ties the pickup to the center: α = a/R. Put together, the ledgers balance as:
(m + I/R²)a = F(1 − r/R).
The profile, 1/50 m², is half of R², 1/25 m², so the mass and the turning count as 2 kg at 3/2: 3 kg at the axle, while 2/5 of 6 N gives 2.4 N on the other side. The center speeds up at 4/5 m/s² →: the spool rolls toward the pull, picking up at 4 sweep a second. The grip is what's left when the cord's 6 N → comes off the 1.6 N → the center needs: f = 1.6 N → + 6 N ← = 4.4 N ←. It pulls back, and about the axle it cranks the spool ↻ harder than the cord cranks it ↺.
(3-d) A spool that stays put has a = 0 and α = 0. The cord still leaves the hub at a tangent, 0.12 m from the axle, so it still cranks the spool ↺ with Fr; only its forward part changes, to → · F. With nothing speeding up, the grip is the same size pointing back, → · F · ←, which read from the other end is ← · F · →: pulling 1/5 m below the axle, it cranks the spool ↺ along with the cord, by (← · F) R. The two turnings add, Fr ↺ + (← · F) R ↺, and with nothing turning that's zero. So the forward part is r/R of the pull, 3/5. That's θ = 53°: the 3–4–5 again, with the 3 along the counter. The spool's inertia never comes into it, because a spool that doesn't pick up never asks for it.
## Drawer a: the kitchen's two 3–4–5s

(a-3) Count about the line where the spool touches the counter. Friction and the counter's push act there, with no leverage about it. (a-4) The bottom line counts what must speed up at the axle: the mass, plus the turning carried as inertia over R². For 3 kg, I/R² = 1 kg, so I = 1/25 kg m², half of mR². That's a uniform disk. Then 6 N at 1 − 3/5 is 2.4 N on 3 kg: a = 4/5 m/s² →, the way the cord pulls. Friction pulls 4.4 N ←.
(a-5) A spool that stays put isn't turning, so the pull has no leverage about the contact. ## 5. The cue ball
(5-a) Call the given impulse J: 2 N s →. The strike is a blow; what it leaves in the center's motion is punch. On 1 kg it leaves the center running at 2 m/s →.
(5-b) Take the lever from the ball's center up to where the cue strikes: (H − R) ↑. The angular momentum a blow leaves about the center is spin, and it's the leverage of the blow, with ↑ × → = ↻:
(H − R) ↑ × J → = (H − R) J ↻.
Rolling at once means the bottom of the ball isn't sliding. The bottom's lever from the center is R ↓, and the turning moves it at the sweep crossed into that lever: ω ↻ × R ↓ = ω R ←. That has to cancel the center's v →, so the ball sweeps at v/R ↻. Its profile about the center is (2/5) R², and (2/5) m R² sweeping at v/R carries
(2/5) m R² (v/R) ↻ = (2/5) R J ↻
of spin, since m v is J. The two senses agree, so the cue went in above the center, and H − R = 2R/5: H = 7R/5 = 0.07 m, two fifths of a radius above the center.
(5-c) The ball sweeps at 2/(1/20) = 40 sweep ↻, and its spin is (2/5)(1/20 m)(2 N s) = 1/25 kg m²/s ↻. The center punches half its velocity dotted into its momentum, and the turning hooks half the sweep dotted into the spin:

½ (40 ↻) · (1/25 kg m²/s ↻) = 4/5 J.

## 9. The double pendulum
(9-a) Each mode cycles with its own sweep, and a turn of it takes T = τ/ω. The slow one takes τ/2.4 = 2.6 s and the fast one τ/5.8 = 1.1 s.
(9-b) The squares are in the ratio (2 + √2)/(2 − √2). Multiply top and bottom by 2 + √2 and it's (2 + √2)²/2, so the sweeps are in the ratio (2 + √2)/√2 = 1 + √2. Flip it and the slow over the fast is √2 − 1: the two multiply to one. That's irrational, so no whole number of slow cycles ever equals a whole number of fast ones, and a motion with both in it never exactly repeats.
(9-c) The modes are the second knife's two tones, and each one's shape is how far the lower rod leans for the upper's lean: Ω_shape = +√2 for the slow tone, both masses swinging the same way, the lower one farther; and −√2 for the fast tone, the lower rod flipped.
The shape is the rods'. The question asks about the masses, and with two of them in one problem it helps to say whose each quantity is.
Call the upper rod with its mass body U, and the lower one body L. A body is the thing as the physics sees it: where it is, how it moves, what it carries, and nothing else. Write the body last in a quantity, after its size and its direction, and read × as “of”: 1 m × U is the length of body U, and 1/10 rad → × U is its swing. The knife of the knife was the knife all along, but the knife of a ladder has nothing to be, and no place to go.
A mass at the end of its rod is out by the rod's length of the rod's swing:
1 m × U × (1/10) rad → × U = 1 m × (1/10) rad → × U × U = 1/10 m → × U × U.
That's 1/10 m forward of body U of body U, which is just body U: the upper mass is out 1/10 m → × U. The lower rod's length of the upper rod's swing, 1 m × L × (1/10) rad → × U, comes to 1/10 m → × L × U, body L of body U.
So the lower mass can't take the upper swing through its own rod. It rides on the upper mass instead: it starts wherever U's mass is, 1/10 m → × L, and its own rod adds its own length of its own swing. In the slow tone the lower rod swings √2 as far the same way, 0.14 rad →:
(1/10) m → × L + 1 m × L × 0.14 rad → × L = 0.24 m → × L.
In a sum the bodies keep their parts apart, the way ← and ↑ do, so the two masses sit at 1/10 m → × U + 0.24 m → × L: both the same way, the lower one 1 + √2 times as far. In the fast tone the lower rod swings √2 as far the other way, 0.14 rad ←:
(1/10) m → × L + 1 m × L × 0.14 rad ← × L = 0.041 m ← × L.
The masses sit at 1/10 m → × U + 0.041 m ← × L. The lower one goes the other way, but only √2 − 1 as far, about 0.41. Its rod leans farther than the upper one, and the mass at its end moves less.
In either tone, each rod picks up at ω² of its own lean, back toward vertical. Both tones at once make a chord, and the chord beats at the gap, 5.8 − 2.4 = 3.4 sweep.
## 10. The coils and the capacitor
(10-a) Say these the right way round. Current is the conductance of the voltage: I = GV, with G = 1/R. Power is the voltage of the current: P = VI. A capacitor is a can: it cans charge, and the charge it cans is the capacitance of its voltage, q = CV. At the start the can holds no charge, so the resistor gets the whole 6 V, and the current is the conductance of 6 V across 20 Ω: 3/10 A. The time constant is RC = 20 × (1/10) = 2 s. When the can's at 4 V, the resistor has the other 2 V, and the current is 1/10 A. The can holds 2/5 C and ½CV² = 4/5 J, and the resistor's burning the voltage of the current, 2 V of 1/10 A, 1/5 W, as heat.
This is a different can, in a separate loop with a coil. A coil coils flux, and the flux it coils is the inductance of its current, Φ = Li.
(10-b) With C = 1/(Lω_a²), LC = 1/ω_a², so ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = ω_a = 2.4 sweep. The loop on its own cycles with the slow mode exactly.
(10-c) The statement writes the torque τ_em; here it's χ_em = κi: the current cranks the rod. The power is the rod's sweep of that torque, κiȧ. The statement's minus in ε = −κȧ is a direction: the rod's motion drives an emf of κȧ back against the current that cranks it, so the circuit gives up the voltage of the current, κȧ of i: the same κiȧ. Whatever the rod gets, the circuit gives up. When the current cranks the rod against its sweep, the energy runs the other way, and the rod's giving its energy to the circuit instead. It's a trade: a belt, not a battery. Nothing's made.
(10-d) (2/3) × 2.4 = 1.6 and (3/2) × 2.4 = 3.6 sweep. Their product is ω₀², 5.9 per second squared, and the faster is 9/4 of the slower: 4 to 9. They're a mirror pair about ω₀: 2/3 and its flip, 3/2. These are the coupled machine's two tones.

## 11. The line
