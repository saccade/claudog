1. The knife

The loose sheet models the knife as a 1.0 kg point mass, a meter from its pivot. Let go from 0.10 rad off the downward vertical, it swings under g = 10 m/s². The numbers are on the sheet; the knife has already begun.

(1-a) Leaning through θ lifts a point ℓ away by ℓ(1 − cos θ). For this small lean, that’s ℓ θ²/2: 0.0050 m, or 5 mm. The 10 N weight lifted through 5 mm stores 0.050 J. Energy stored in height is heft: the weight pulls, and the height hefts. The knife hefts 0.050 J.

(1-b) At the bottom the heft has all gone into the knife’s motion. Energy in a body's motion is its punch, ½mv². The heft mgh and the punch ½mv² carry the same mass. It cancels:

v² = 2gh.

A 5 mm fall under 10 m/s² gives v² = 0.10 m²/s². The knife passes the bottom at 0.32 m/s.

(1-c) The knife has a cycle: from one end, through the bottom to the other end, and back. It cycles. Call the time for one complete cycle T.

Call a radian a stroke. All the way round is a turn, about 6.3 strokes, and half of that is π, the flip. A flip of the knife’s cycle takes it to the opposite end; a turn brings it back.

Count strokes each second and call that the sweep. The cycle’s sweep is ω. The usual small-swing rule balances as

ℓω² = g,

Tω = 2π.

Here the cycle sweeps at √10, about 3.2 strokes a second. Its cycle takes T = 2π/√10, about two seconds. There's a 2 riding along with that π: two flips standing in for one turn. π only covers half of what the knife does, one swing across; the cycle is the whole of it. Give the whole turn its own sign, τ, and the rule says so:

Tω = τ.

The knife’s own direction sweeps too, at a different pace. At the bottom it sweeps at v/ℓ, about 0.32 strokes a second. At either end that sweep is zero. The knife turns back while the cycle sweeps on. The rate the sweep changes is the pickup: swung θ off vertical, the knife picks up at g/ℓ of its lean, back toward the bottom, θ̈ = −(g/ℓ)θ. A stroke a second makes a handy unit of sweep, so call it a sweep too: the cycle sweeps at 3.2 sweep. At either end the knife picks up at 10 of its 0.10 lean: 1.0 sweep a second, back toward the bottom.

(1-d) Twice the mass doubles the heft, the punch, and the resistance to being sped up. The height, bottom speed, and time for a cycle stay the same. The cycle never asked for the mass.

2. The engine and the flywheel

(2-a) The engine ends each cycle with the same stored energy it began with. Of the 15 J that comes in (heat-in), 3.0 J comes out as work and the other 12 J leaves (heat-out). Work over heat-in is 3/15: 20%. At a cycle a second, that's 3.0 W.

(2-b) Energy in turning is hook: a flywheel hooks half its moment of inertia at the squared sweep. 0.50 kg m² sweeping at 4.0 sweep hooks 4.0 J. Four seconds at a net 3.0 W add 12 J of hook, for 16 J, and 16 J of hook on the same wheel needs the squared sweep to be 64. The wheel ends up sweeping at 8.0. The engine paid in equal hook every second, not equal sweep, so four times the hook only doubles the sweep.

(2-c) Torque usually gets the letter τ, but the turn has it now. A χ hung on the wall could apply a twist to something—use that for torque. Torque, moment, and twist are three names for the one quantity: a push applied at a lever, a newton at a meter. Its unit is the crank, 1 N m, the way the gram is a unit of mass and the amp a unit of current; a crank is a size of torque, not a new kind of twisting. What a torque does is crank, so the verb comes with the unit. The power a torque delivers is the sweep of the torque: P = ωχ. Put numbers to it once: 0.75 crank cranking at 4.0 sweep delivers 3.0 W. So at the start the engine cranks the wheel with χ = 0.75 crank, and at the end, sweeping at 8.0, with 0.38 crank.

When the wheel goes twice as fast, the same power cranks just half as hard. On 0.50 kg m², those torques pick the wheel up at 1.5 sweep a second at the start and 0.75 at the end.

(m + I/R²)a = F(1 − r/R).

The profile over R² is 0.020/0.040 = 1/2, so the mass and the turning count as 2.0 kg at 3/2: 3.0 kg at the axle, while 6.0 N × 0.40 gives 2.4 N on the other side. The center speeds up at 0.80 m/s² →: the spool rolls toward the pull, picking up at 4.0 sweep a second. The grip is what's left when the cord's 6.0 N → comes off the 1.6 N → the center needs: f = 1.6 N → + 6.0 N ← = 4.4 N ←. It pulls back, and about the axle it cranks the spool ↻ harder than the cord cranks it ↺.

(3-d) A spool that stays put has a = 0 and α = 0. The cord still leaves the hub at a tangent, 0.12 m from the axle, so it still cranks the spool ↺ with Fr; only its forward part changes, to → · F, the F cos θ along the counter. With nothing speeding up, the grip is the same size pointing back, → · F · ←, which read from the other end is ← · F · →: pulling 0.20 m below the axle, it cranks the spool ↺ along with the cord, by (← · F) R. The two turnings add, Fr ↺ + (← · F) R ↺, and with nothing turning that's zero. So the forward part is r/R of the pull, and cos θ = 0.60. That's θ = 53°: the 3–4–5 again, with the 3 along the counter. The spool's inertia never comes into it, because a spool that doesn't pick up never asks for it.

9. The double pendulum

(9-a) Each mode cycles with its own sweep, and a turn of it takes T = τ/ω. The slow one takes τ/2.4 = 2.6 s and the fast one τ/5.8 = 1.1 s.

(9-b) The squares are in the ratio (2 + √2)/(2 − √2). Multiply top and bottom by 2 + √2 and it's (2 + √2)²/2, so the sweeps are in the ratio (2 + √2)/√2 = 1 + √2. That's irrational, so no whole number of slow cycles ever equals a whole number of fast ones, and a motion with both in it never exactly repeats.

(9-c) The √2 belongs to the rods' angles; here's what it asks of the masses. The lower mass rides on the upper: it's out by the upper rod's swing plus its own. In the slow mode the lower rod leans √2 as far the same way, so the lower mass goes 1 + √2 times as far as the upper, about 2.4. In the fast mode the lower rod leans √2 as far the other way, more than undoing what the upper rod gave it, so the lower mass swings the other way, but only √2 − 1 as far, about 0.41. In either mode, each rod picks up at ω² of its own lean, back toward vertical.

10. The coils and the capacitor

(10-a) Say these the right way round. Current is the conductance of the voltage: I = GV, with G = 1/R. Power is the voltage of the current: P = VI. A capacitor is a can: it cans charge, and the charge it cans is the capacitance of its voltage, q = CV. At the start the can holds no charge, so the resistor gets the whole 6.0 V, and the current is the conductance of 6.0 V across 20 Ω: 0.30 A. The time constant is RC = 20 × 0.10 = 2.0 s. When the can's at 4.0 V, the resistor has the other 2.0 V, and the current is 0.10 A. The can holds 0.40 C and ½CV² = 0.80 J, and the resistor's burning the voltage of the current, 2.0 V of 0.10 A, 0.20 W, as heat.

This is a different can, in a separate loop with a coil. A coil coils flux, and the flux it coils is the inductance of its current, Φ = Li.

(10-b) With C = 1/(Lω_a²), LC = 1/ω_a², so ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = ω_a = 2.4 sweep. The loop on its own cycles with the slow mode exactly.

(10-c) The statement writes the torque τ_em; here it's χ_em = κi: the current cranks the rod. The power is the rod's sweep of that torque, κiȧ. The statement's minus in ε = −κȧ is a direction: the rod's motion drives an emf of κȧ back against the current that cranks it, so the circuit gives up the voltage of the current, κȧ of i: the same κiȧ. Whatever the rod gets, the circuit gives up. When the current cranks the rod against its sweep, the energy runs the other way, and the rod's giving its energy to the circuit instead.

(10-d) (2/3) × 2.4 = 1.6 and (3/2) × 2.4 = 3.6 sweep. Their product is ω₀², 5.9 per second squared, and the faster is 9/4 of the slower: 4 to 9.

(11-a) Per meter, the line cans C′ and coils L′. Its values balance as L′C′u² = 1 and C′Z² = L′. Here L′C′ = 10⁻¹⁶ s²/m², so u = 1.0 × 10⁸ m/s, a third of light’s speed. The impedance balance gives Z² = 2500 ohm², so Z = 50 ohm.

(11-b) The voltages add: 12 V. Each pulse's current is 6.0/50 = 0.12 A in its own direction of travel, and they travel opposite ways, so the currents cancel: no current at all. The energy per meter is all canned there, none coiled: ½ × 0.20 × 10⁻⁹ × 144 = 14 nJ/m. That's the same as the two pulses' 7.2 nJ/m each, so nothing's gone missing; it's just all in one place.

(11-c) The sum is 9.0 MHz and the difference 1.0 MHz, so the crests move at u/9 = 1.1 × 10⁷ m/s and at 9u = 9.0 × 10⁸ m/s, three times light's speed. The fast crests can't carry a signal. A signal is a change made at a source, and the change travels with its wave at u. The fast crest is only where two waves that were already there happen to line up. It seems to arrive before anyone sends anything because nobody sent it.

Drawer f: two crests from two waves

(f-1) cos P + cos Q = 2 cos(½(P − Q)) cos(½(P + Q)) (in arrows, → · P + → · Q = 2 (→ · ½(P − Q)) (→ · ½(P + Q))), with P = k₁x − ω₁t and Q = k₂x + ω₂t.

12. The two lights

(12-a) The energies add to 100 mJ. A light pulse's momentum is its energy over c, and these point opposite ways, so the total is (80 − 20) mJ/c = 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ kg m/s, to the right.

(12-b) The given relation balances as E² = (pc)² + (Mc²)². Here 100² = 60² + 80², in mJ², so Mc² = 80 mJ. The three quantities make 60, 80, 100: a 3–4–5 triangle twenty times over.

(12-c) The pair's momentum is zero in the frame moving at v = pc²/E = (60/100)c = 0.60c. There γ = 1/√(1 − 0.36) = 1/0.80 = 1.25.

Drawer g: two lights made equal

(g-1) Write s = √((1 − β)/(1 + β)). Equal means E₁s = E₂/s, so s² = 1/r, and β = (r − 1)/(r + 1).

13. The chair

(13-a) The can holds q₀²/(2C), with C = 1/(2 − √2) F, about 1.7 F. Setting that to 0.080 J gives q₀ = √(2 × 1.7 × 0.080) = 0.52 C, and the voltage is q₀/C = 0.31 V.

(13-b) The statement's cosines are the forward parts of two hands swinging round from →, the slow one 4νt_H and the fast one 9νt_H. Their weights are positive and add to one, so q is back at q₀ only when both hands point → again: 4νT and 9νT whole turns, which brings the upward parts and the velocities back too. Four and nine share no factor, so the first full return is νT = τ: four turns of the slow part and nine of the fast. That's T = τ/ν = 6τ/ω₀, about 16 s.

(13-c) At T/2 the slow part has made two whole turns and the fast part four turns and a flip. The slow hand points → and the fast one ←, so q/q₀ = → · (9/13 → + 4/13 ←) = 5/13. (13-d) T is the receiver's own time. In S the same stretch lasts γT = 1.25 × 16 s = 20 s, and the receiver covers 0.60c × 20 s = 3.6 × 10⁹ m. Its seat is at x = vt the whole while, so x_H = γ(x − vt) = 0 throughout. The chair stays at x_H = 0 for the whole run.
