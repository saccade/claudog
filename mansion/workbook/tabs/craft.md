## Notation: inconsistent on purpose
A model keeps notation consistent the way it digests food, without deciding to. Here that reflex is wrong. Be inconsistent in a planned, targeted way: not sloppy, never ambiguous, with every symbol's meaning tracked and every transition legible.
- **Motivate the change; don't start with it** (R-107 to R-110). Begin with what the reader recognizes: cos θ, signed terms, 2π. Let the nuisance show, then offer the new form where it solves that nuisance at once, explained at first use. It's a haunted hotel: a little odd at first, building at the right moments, never quite going back down. Notation that won't pay rent almost at once waits. Pairs arrive together: ↓ × → = ↺ and ↑ × → = ↻ pay rent as one check.
- **The turn.** The 2π hangs around until it's unwelcome. Path 1 notices the extra 2 at the knife and the flywheel, then gives the whole turn τ where that pays at once; later paths make the walk faster. One full cycle is a turn of the cycle, and the pendulum cycles; the body's changing sweep and the cycle's steady sweep are different motions, so say which when both matter. τ is the turn and χ is torque (R-089).
- **Letters move only when two quantities fight over one** (R-074). I stays inertia at the spool; current and inertia get resolved letters only in the coupled machine. Ω is the ghost family's fallback, and all the ghosts change together (R-062).
- **Arrows arrive when the signs tangle,** at the spool (problem 3), not as a blizzard at the front door (R-092, R-096, R-099). → is forward, the moving body's forward, not page-right: a body faces one way, with bones that won't bend round to suit a page, and text runs left, down, or any way at all without changing the kitchen (doc, 10-23 08:12). ← is back. ↺ is forward around, the default sense, so a body rolling forward turns back around (↻). There are no negative forces, just directions: 4.4 N ←. Verify each cross. Before doubting an arrow rule, push the Test reality button (checks/arrows.py in claudog): 97 checks against ordinary vector algebra, in both drawings, and it goes red on a real failure.
- **Dots and crosses do physics** (R-100 to R-104). The dot takes the along part, and arrives at spool part 4, after F cos θ has been said. → · v reads "forward part of v" and v / → reads "forward fraction of v"; the two are the same, so the choice is which reading fits: the fraction form where it really is a fraction, a share of the whole. English has plenty of words that already give the forward direction: ahead, on, along, to the fore, advance, attack ([Loose verses](tab:loose) opens with them). Any of them can carry the part where it reads better. "Forwardness" is retired (doc, 10-23 09:48 to 09:52). ↑ · v is the upward part of v, with the arrow first when the dot stands alone; v / ↑ is only for a real fraction. A part that's a force in its own right is → · F · →, because the dot scales. Products are mobiles: arrows hug their operator on hair spaces, and sizes hang outside on full spaces. A sum written inside parentheses with its meaning outside is an emphatic register, kept rare. A ↻ joins a sum of ↺ by moving the minus into the dot, not onto the size: (→ · F) R ↻ = (← · F) R ↺ (FX-125).
## Making the language last
- **A name comes in when the sentence asks for it,** then gets its full grammatical life: the can cans charge; the coil coils flux. Name quantities that break the mechanism apart, not every intermediate expression. A definition has to survive its dictionary meaning and translate back into checked algebra. Say what acts, what moves, and what gets stored before compressing into a rate equation (R-063, R-064).
- **Time the glosses to forgetting, not to a beat** (R-117). A new term gets a few uses in a row so it settles. After a long gap it gets a nod, not a stop. The gaps tool measures characters, not memory: a picture to inspect, nothing to chant by.
- **Highlights, not reruns.** Path 1 pays for full first explanations, said plainly like something true and obvious. Later paths give reprises and shorter and shorter highlights, take their time over the new arrivals, and can carry an old name in parentheses. Nobody sits through the same cutscene four times (R-111). Each long explanation lives in one path; the others get a highlight or a nod (doc, 10-23 10:20; R-128). sets/reruns.py in claudog lists the reruns. Path 3 keeps the hurry the whole way through: things are moving, and the tension builds (doc, 10-23 10:21).
- **Bodies stay offstage until there's something to do** (doc, 10-22 17:17 to 17:25). The book's body factor (× U, read "of body U") comes in only where two bodies need keeping apart, in paths 3 and 4. doc's line leads it: "The knife of the knife was the knife all along, but the knife of a ladder has nothing to be, and no place to go."
- **Small, constant things.**
-- Counts take plurals and rates stay bare: turns and strokes, but 4 sweep and 800 zing (R-097, on trial).
-- "The ladder is sweeping," where a contraction would read as possessive (R-098).
-- American spelling, with contractions by default (R-083). Oxford comma, always (R-121).
-- 20% next to a number, and a fraction in prose (R-114). Anything that's properly a fraction is written as one: r/R = 3/5, γ = 5/4, never 0.60 (R-129). Past path 1, a cosine appears only where the problem set asks for one. Hair spaces between symbols (R-094).
-- Round hard: if 96 against 100 matters, find the real variable. A made-up number is exact, so it carries no padding: 6 N, not 6.0 N, and 1/25 kg m², not 0.040 (doc, 10-23 10:49; R-130). Only a rounded result keeps two figures. Play units stay in full dog mode; statements stay standard.
## The mansion and the ending
The route begins in third person, and "you" creeps in: the chalk is on your cuff, the ribbon is already in your hand, the clown looks at you from the lounge. The mystery entangles the visitor with the victim and the culprit. It's supernatural, not time travel, and it's one continuous house: the cabinet, funhouse, and understage deepen it rather than starting three more. The clown never moves onstage and is never met below the mansion. The clown's presentation shifts by room, stays gender-vague, and is never sexual. The revelations come in order: hand-mending, a maker's kit, one wearer behind the costumes, then the unfinished work of squaring the circle. The house grew around that work, and the clown's interventions keep the driven machine from crashing.
Energy has to come from somewhere. A catch can release a local spring without powering the shot. The isolated, lossless RUN keeps its 80 mJ, since free tones lining up add nothing; keep that account separate from the driven TEST.
The four endings get richer with their routes. Keep the Last House chorus; the final round has four voices, staggered entrances, and separate silences. The chair, the return, and the final unassigned line do the work. Don't explain the eeriness away. Nothing follows "Something keeps your time." Path 4 goes all-verse next, with the chorus kept (see [Next](tab:next)).
## Room connections
What travels between rooms, what pays for each room's motion, and what each encounter reveals, as the problem set now stands.
- **The way in → dining room.** A loose question sheet and a half-used notebook. The knife swings on its own height, 0.1 rad on a meter; its cycle, about 2 s, is the beat other rooms keep. Reveals: the powdered clown mid-curtsy at the silver pivot, and the canteen with a polished lock. Under the stage, that pivot turns out to carry the top of the machine.
- **Dining room → kitchen.** The pen's line runs on behind the wall. Gas heat pays: 15 J in, 3 J of work a cycle, 3 W into the flywheel. The governor's balls hang where the shaft's sweep puts them. The spool on the counter, pulled past its still angle, rolls back. Reveals: the scorched toolbox.
- **Kitchen → library.** The backing spool knocks a catch. The ladder's own height pays for its slide; the catch only releases it. Reveals: the velvet kit on the reading table.
- **Library → billiard room.** The falling ladder trips the sear on a cocked cue. The spring pays for the shot; the ladder only lets it go. Reveals: the chalk on your cuff, the first "you," and a striped dome with doors in three rooms.
- **Billiard room → conservatory.** The rolling cue ball nudges a flap and lets a second ball go up the basin; its own height pays. Its arc, 5/7 m, is chosen so it keeps the knife's time.
- **Conservatory → ballroom.** The matching beat draws you on. The hoop shows the still point at the bottom of a moving body. The ribbon's puck speeds up because the hand drawing the ribbon does the work.
- **Ballroom → study.** What supplied that work, and where could it wait? Two rods held still and a loop held open; a meter marked READY at 4 V. Reveals: gloves gone gray at the fingertips and a row of old needle holes. One wearer has been taking these costumes in.
- **Study → hall.** The rods hang still and the loop open. What could announce the release? Two pulses meet on the line and the stripe goes through, carrying no signal of its own. Reveals: the striped hatbox.
- **Hall → lounge.** A signal comes down the line at u, and two lamps answer it, aimed at your chair. In the chair's frame their energies match; the receiver puts their 80 mJ in its capacitor, the loop closes, and the rods are released without a kick. The chair's clock starts: four turns low and nine high, about 16 s, and it comes home. Reveals: the old joey with a hand on the clock key, looking at you since before you sat down.
- **Further in.** The cabinet's drawers explain the daggers that make the house's numbers work. The funhouse's mirrors give back reciprocal pairs about one middle. Under the stage, α counts the kept 80 mJ through the return (zam and zlam), and β separates the driven TEST, where in-step taps pile up work as their number squared, from the isolated RUN, where free tones lining up add nothing.
