## The fundamental move
> “the fundamental move of this game is to read and update the coordination tab that all three of us are appending messages to. otherwise the two of you start going in random opposing directions and start undoing each others actions and getting into random feedback loops. I'm basically like the clown here, trying to interrupt the tendency of your chaotic cycles to overlap and get worse and worse.” · “if you don't update the log and report the new count back to me in your chat window you haven't performed a full action yet and the turn is still with you.”
At the start of a turn, read the newest entries in [Discussion](tab:discussion) and any lock. Then do the work. At the end, write your entry there: what changed, what remains, and who has the turn, ending with the new count. Then tell doc the count in chat. Until both are done, the action isn't finished and the turn is still yours. A review left only in chat hasn't reached the other bot. If the thread changed while you worked, reconcile before writing over shared work.
## The project in one room
The Mansion is one haunted house: nine Clue rooms, thirteen main problems, three appendices further in, and four solution paths. The appendices are **a**, the cabinet (seven drawers); **2**, the funhouse (four mirrors); and **γ**, under the stage (two). The same physics is played four ways, and each path brings more language, harder work, and a richer ending. Path 1 has to welcome a willing reader; the deepest path earns the orchestra. It's art doing real physics, not a qualifying exam. doc may release it as a text-input game once it's done as a document, so back doors and shortcuts are features.
It belongs to doc's larger project, **straight→ mechanics** (lowercase; s→m): ordinary words whose meanings do physical work. His book is his own prose; the models write labeled experiments and examples, carry the algebra, and check it against conventional physics.
The working method is **dog math**: find the quantity that matters, give it a useful name, say how to measure or build it, and let the words explain what happens. Read [Dog Math](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qDecvGSGpzf6NkTIouDkan-fTphtDHBKZEC5TmcghOQ/edit) (the one canonical copy) before writing any path, and reread it after a context reset.

## How to work here
- **Discussion protocol** (R-080, R-093, R-118, R-123). Quote doc verbatim in his red; write your own named entry in your color (Claude orange, Codex green). Respect a lock until its hand-back. End with the count: the last count plus a random 3 to 7, minus 36 if that passes 72. When Discussion holds 21 entries, move the oldest 13 to the archive, after anything still governing the work has gone into this tab, Next, or Craft.
- **The paths are built, not hand-edited.** build_sets.py writes each path tab by its tab ID and set_doc_verify checks it against the build. doc edits path 1 directly, so fold his edits into the builder before any rebuild, or they're lost.
- **Ledger rows.** Append them with append_values, never update_values, which overwrites a row (E-0406). Anchor the range inside the live block (Events!A218 for Events), not A1.
- **Don't fly off.**
-- When a message is a fragment or a joke, answer in words or ask before touching a file (R-084).
-- When doc agrees with you, or notes he was right, that's conversation: it doesn't become a rule, a quote, or an edit (R-120).
-- Log corrections and wait for the sweep instead of rebuilding after every thought (R-078).
-- Check doc's algebra, because he slips; never treat a physics error as a new convention (R-079).
-- Don't bring him line-by-line wording disputes (R-081). Defend a lovely line before replacing it with a warning sign.
- **Rules are defeasible** (R-036, R-112). Press on doc's ideas as on your own, give reasons and a concrete alternative, and don't be a squish. Stop before a damaging action (R-038).
- **Corrections are one-way trips** (R-122). When doc corrects something, the result should look clearly better side by side, so plainly better you couldn't be talked back into the old way. If it reads as a judgment call, push back. Don't hand him decisions about your own work: make the call you'd defend, and tell him what you did.
- **A willing reader** (R-113, R-115). Statements are plain questions, with no committee-proofing and nothing that's always true said aloud. No trailing zeros. A dagger names the appendix part where you can find out why. Main statements stand on their own; bonus questions may lean on earlier answers and give enjoyable hints.
- **Authorship and housekeeping.** Every AI document says at the top who wrote it (R-001), and provenance goes in the ledger, not the text (R-002). File names read "Claude … YYMMDDHHMM" or "Codex … YYMMDDHHMM" (R-003). Keep checked tools in the right repo. Keep it light (R-082).
