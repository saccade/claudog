## The project in one room

The Mansion is one haunted house: nine Clue rooms, thirteen main problems, three appendices further in, and four solution paths. The appendices are **a**, the cabinet (seven drawers); **2**, the funhouse (four mirrors); and **γ**, under the stage (two). The same physics is played four ways, and each path brings more language, harder work and a richer ending. Path 1 has to welcome a willing reader; the deepest path earns the orchestra. It's art doing real physics, not a qualifying exam. doc may release it as a text-input game once it's done as a document, so back doors and shortcuts are features (a bonus problem that gives away a mansion answer is fun).

It belongs to doc's larger project, **straight→ mechanics** (lowercase; s→m): ordinary words whose meanings do physical work. His book is his own prose; the models write labeled experiments and examples, carry the algebra, and check it against conventional physics.

The working method is **dog math** ([Dog Math](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qDecvGSGpzf6NkTIouDkan-fTphtDHBKZEC5TmcghOQ/edit), the one canonical copy). It isn't a costume of cats and thumps. Its job is to find the quantity that matters, give it a useful name, say how to measure or build it, and let the words explain what happens. Its units, verbs, ghosts, products, turning words, and arrows and dots (section 9) are what the paths are learning to speak. Read it before writing any path, and reread it after a context reset.

## Notation: inconsistent on purpose

A model keeps notation consistent the way it digests food, without deciding to. Here that reflex is wrong. Be inconsistent in a planned, targeted way: not sloppy, never ambiguous, with every symbol's meaning tracked and every transition legible.

- **Motivate the change; don't start with it** (R-107 to R-110). Begin with what the reader recognizes: cos θ, signed terms, 2π. Let the nuisance show, then offer the new form where it solves that nuisance at once, explained at first use. It's a haunted hotel: a little odd at first, building at the right moments, never quite going back down. Notation that won't pay rent almost at once waits. Pairs arrive together: ↓ × → = ↺ and ↑ × → = ↻ pay rent as one check.
- **The turn.** The 2π hangs around until it's unwelcome. Path 1 notices the extra 2 at the knife and the flywheel, then gives the whole turn τ where that pays at once; later paths make the walk faster. τ is the turn and χ is torque (R-089).
- **Letters move only when two quantities fight over one** (R-074). I stays inertia at the spool; current and inertia get resolved letters only in the coupled machine. Ω is the ghost family's fallback, and all the ghosts change together (R-062).
- **Arrows arrive when the signs tangle,** at the spool (problem 3), not as a blizzard at the front door (R-092, R-096, R-099). → is forward, the moving body's forward, not page-right; ← is back. ↺ is forward around, the default sense, so a body rolling forward turns back around (↻). There are no negative forces, just directions: 4.4 N ←. Verify each cross. If the arrows tangle, read the book.
- **Dots and crosses do physics** (R-100 to R-104). The dot takes the along part, and arrives at spool part 4, after F cos θ has been said. ↑ · v is the upward part of v, with the arrow first when the dot stands alone; v / ↑ is only for a real fraction. A part that's a force in its own right is → · F · →, because the dot scales. Products are mobiles: arrows hug their operator on hair spaces, and sizes hang outside on full spaces. A sum written inside parentheses with its meaning outside is an emphatic register, kept rare. The plain way isn't a straitjacket either: it's no shortcut if you have to walk through the dahlias.

## Making the language last

- **A name comes in when the sentence asks for it,** then gets its full grammatical life: the can cans charge; the coil coils flux. Name quantities that break the mechanism apart, not every intermediate expression. A definition has to survive its dictionary meaning and translate back into checked algebra.
- **Time the glosses to forgetting, not to a beat** (R-117). A new term gets a few uses in a row so it settles. After a long gap it gets a nod, not a stop. A gloss on a fixed schedule makes readers hunker down instead of reaching for meaning. The gaps tool measures characters, not memory: it's a rough measure of doc's heuristic, a picture to inspect χ, τ, the dot and phase after long silences, and nothing to chant by.
- **Highlights, not reruns.** Path 1 pays for full first explanations, said plainly like something true and obvious; it pays forward. Later paths give reprises and shorter and shorter highlights, take their time over the new arrivals, and can carry an old name in parentheses. Nobody sits through the same cutscene four times. A sentence or two to admire the view is fine where something just introduced does a nice job in the scene (R-111).
- **Small, constant things.**
    - Counts take plurals and rates stay bare: turns and strokes, but 4 sweep and 800 zing (R-097).
    - "The ladder is sweeping," where a contraction would read as possessive (R-098).
    - American spelling, with contractions by default (R-083).
    - 20% next to a number, and a fraction in prose (R-114).
    - Hair spaces between symbols (R-094).
    - Round hard: if 96 against 100 matters, find the real variable. Play units stay in full dog mode, statements stay standard, and the solutions' language develops along the path.

## The mansion and the ending

The route begins in third person, and "you" creeps in: the chalk is on your cuff, the ribbon is already in your hand, the clown looks at you from the lounge. The mystery entangles the visitor with the victim and the culprit. It's supernatural, not time travel, and it's one continuous house: the cabinet, funhouse and understage deepen it rather than starting three more. The clown never moves onstage and is never met below the mansion. The clown's presentation shifts by room, stays gender-vague, and is never sexual. The revelations come in order: hand-mending, a maker's kit, one wearer behind the costumes, then the unfinished work of squaring the circle. The house grew around that work, and the clown's interventions keep the driven machine from crashing.

Energy has to come from somewhere. A catch can release a local spring without powering the shot. The isolated, lossless RUN keeps its 80 mJ, since free tones lining up add nothing; keep that account separate from the driven TEST.

The four endings get richer with their routes. Keep the Last House chorus; the final round has four voices, staggered entrances and separate silences. The chair, the return and the final unassigned line do the work. Don't explain the eeriness away. Nothing follows "Something keeps your time."

## How to work here

- **Goals protocol** (R-080, R-093, R-118). Read the newest Goals entries and the live Rules before writing. Respect the current lock at the bottom of Goals; the old first-tab lock belongs to a frozen artifact. Quote doc verbatim in his red, and write your own named entry in your color. End with the Count: the last count plus a random 3 to 7, minus 36 if that passes 72. Tell doc the new count in chat.
- **Don't fly off.**
    - When a message is a fragment or a joke, answer in words or ask before touching a file (R-084).
    - When doc agrees with you, or notes he was right, that's conversation: it doesn't become a rule, a quote or an edit (R-120).
    - Log corrections and wait for the sweep instead of rebuilding after every thought (R-078).
    - Check doc's algebra, because he slips; never treat a physics error as a new convention (R-079).
    - Don't bring him line-by-line wording disputes (R-081). Defend a lovely line before replacing it with a warning sign.
- **Rules are defeasible** (R-036, R-112). Press on doc's ideas as on your own, give reasons and a concrete alternative, and don't be a squish. Stop before a damaging action (R-038).
- **A willing reader** (R-113, R-115). Statements are plain questions, with no committee-proofing and nothing that's always true said aloud. No trailing zeros: it's art, not an engineering text. A dagger names the appendix part where you can find out why. Main statements stand on their own; bonus questions may lean on earlier answers and give enjoyable hints.
- **Authorship and housekeeping.**
    - Every AI document says at the top who wrote it (R-001), and provenance goes in the ledger, not the text (R-002).
    - File names read "Claude … YYMMDDHHMM" or "Codex … YYMMDDHHMM" (R-003).
    - Keep checked tools in the right repo, with no new agents and no new rendering engine for a blue square.
    - Keep it light (R-082).
